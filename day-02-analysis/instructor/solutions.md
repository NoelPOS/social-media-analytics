### Exercise 1: Trend Analysis Workshop

**Dataset 1: Monthly Followers**
```
Jan: 5,000
Feb: 5,100
Mar: 5,200
Apr: 5,300
May: 5,400
Jun: 5,500
```

**Solution:**
- **Trend Type:** Linear Growth ✓
- **Calculation:** +100 followers per month consistently
- **Growth Rate:** 10% over 6 months (500/5,000 = 10%)
- **Insight:** "Steady, predictable growth at 100 followers/month"
- **Action:** "Current strategy is working. Continue course. Monitor for plateau."
- **Extrapolation:** If trend continues → 5,600 in July, 5,700 in August

**Teaching Points:**
- Linear = same absolute amount each period
- Predictable but may plateau
- Sustainable growth rate

---

**Dataset 2: Weekly Engagement Rate**
```
Week 1: 5.2%
Week 2: 5.8%
Week 3: 6.7%
Week 4: 8.1%
Week 5: 10.4%
Week 6: 13.8%
```

**Solution:**
- **Trend Type:** Exponential Growth ✓
- **Calculation:** 
  - Week 1-2: +0.6%
  - Week 2-3: +0.9%
  - Week 3-4: +1.4%
  - Week 4-5: +2.3%
  - Week 5-6: +3.4%
  - Increases are accelerating!
- **Growth Rate:** 165% over 6 weeks (13.8/5.2 = 2.65x)
- **Insight:** "Exponential acceleration suggests viral content or algorithm boost"
- **Action:** "Identify what's driving growth. Replicate successful content. This won't last forever - exponential growth always slows."
- **Warning:** "Unsustainable long-term. Enjoy it while it lasts!"

**Teaching Points:**
- Exponential = growth rate increasing
- Often from viral effect
- Temporary phenomenon
- Critical to identify cause

---

**Dataset 3: Quarterly Post Reach**
```
Q1: 50,000
Q2: 65,000
Q3: 45,000
Q4: 60,000
Q1: 48,000
Q2: 68,000
```

**Solution:**
- **Trend Type:** Seasonal Pattern ✓
- **Pattern Recognition:**
  - Q1: Average 49,000 (50K + 48K / 2)
  - Q2: Average 66,500 (65K + 68K / 2)
  - Q3: 45,000 (one data point)
  - Q4: 60,000 (one data point)
  - Clear peaks in Q2 and Q4
- **Insight:** "Cyclical pattern with Q2 and Q4 peaks, Q3 valleys. Likely business-related (e.g., retail: holiday seasons, summer lull)."
- **Action:** 
  - "Plan heavy campaigns for Q2 and Q4"
  - "Maintain presence in Q3 but adjust expectations"
  - "Budget accordingly for seasonal fluctuations"
- **Prediction:** Q3 this year ≈ 45,000 reach, Q4 ≈ 60,000+

**Teaching Points:**
- Seasonal patterns repeat
- Common in retail, B2B, education
- Plan around, don't fight them
- Use historical data to predict future

### Exercise 2: Comparative Analysis

**Data:**
| Account | Followers | Avg. Likes | Avg. Comments | Avg. Shares | Avg. Post Reach |
|---------|-----------|------------|---------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Coffee A | 25,000 | 800 | 40 | 15 | 12,000 |
| Coffee B | 15,000 | 750 | 60 | 25 | 10,000 |
| Coffee C | 35,000 | 900 | 30 | 10 | 18,000 |

---

**Task 1: Calculate Total Engagements**

**Coffee A:**
```
Likes + Comments + Shares
= 800 + 40 + 15
= 855 total engagements
```

**Coffee B:**
```
750 + 60 + 25
= 835 total engagements
```

**Coffee C:**
```
900 + 30 + 10
= 940 total engagements
```

---

**Task 2: Calculate Engagement Rate**

**Formula:** (Total Engagements / Reach) × 100

**Coffee A:**
```
(855 / 12,000) × 100
= 0.07125 × 100
= 7.125% or 7.13%
```

**Coffee B:**
```
(835 / 10,000) × 100
= 0.0835 × 100
= 8.35%
```

**Coffee C:**
```
(940 / 18,000) × 100
= 0.0522 × 100
= 5.22%
```

---

**Task 3: Calculate Engagement per 1,000 Followers**

**Formula:** (Total Engagements / Followers) × 1,000

**Coffee A:**
```
(855 / 25,000) × 1,000
= 0.0342 × 1,000
= 34.2 per 1K followers
```

**Coffee B:**
```
(835 / 15,000) × 1,000
= 0.0557 × 1,000
= 55.7 per 1K followers
```

**Coffee C:**
```
(940 / 35,000) × 1,000
= 0.0269 × 1,000
= 26.9 per 1K followers
```

---

**Task 4: Rankings**

**By Engagement Rate:**
1. 🥇 Coffee B - 8.35%
2. 🥈 Coffee A - 7.13%
3. 🥉 Coffee C - 5.22%

**By Engagement per 1K Followers:**
1. 🥇 Coffee B - 55.7
2. 🥈 Coffee A - 34.2
3. 🥉 Coffee C - 26.9

**By Total Engagements (raw numbers):**
1. Coffee C - 940
2. Coffee A - 855
3. Coffee B - 835

---

**Task 5: Winner & Explanation**

**Winner: Coffee B** 🏆

**Explanation:**
"Coffee B wins despite having the smallest following and lowest total engagement count. They have the highest engagement rate (8.35%) and highest per-capita engagement (55.7 per 1K followers), indicating they've built a highly engaged community. Quality of audience > quantity of followers."

**Key Insight:**
"Coffee C has the most followers and raw engagements but the lowest rates. Their audience is less engaged. They're reaching many people but not connecting deeply."

**Recommendations:**

**Coffee A:** "Solid performance across the board. Continue current strategy. Look to Coffee B for engagement tactics."

**Coffee B:** "Excellent engagement! You've built a loyal community. Now focus on growth while maintaining quality. Consider collaborations, ads, or hashtag strategies to expand reach."

**Coffee C:** "You have scale but lack engagement. Audit content quality. Are you posting for followers or for likes? Shift to more engaging, interactive content. Consider surveys, questions, user-generated content."

### Activity 4: Chart Selection Challenge

**Solutions with Detailed Reasoning:**

---

**Scenario 1:** Track Instagram followers from Jan-Jun
- **Best Answer:** LINE CHART ✓
- **Why:** Time series data, continuous trend, shows growth trajectory
- **Alternative:** Area chart (shows cumulative feel)
- **Wrong:** Bar chart (discrete categories feel, though technically works)
- **Wrong:** Pie chart (not parts of a whole)

**What it shows:** Follower growth trajectory over 6 months
**Example insight:** "Steady growth with spike in March"

---

**Scenario 2:** Compare engagement rates across 4 platforms (Instagram, Facebook, Twitter, TikTok)
- **Best Answer:** BAR CHART ✓
- **Why:** Comparing discrete categories, easy to see which is highest
- **Alternative:** Horizontal bar chart (same thing)
- **Acceptable:** Column chart (vertical bars)
- **Wrong:** Line chart (platforms aren't continuous data)
- **Wrong:** Pie chart (not parts of a whole - rates don't sum to 100%)

**What it shows:** Which platform has best engagement
**Example insight:** "TikTok engagement 2x higher than Facebook"

---

**Scenario 3:** Show age distribution of audience (18-24: 40%, 25-34: 35%, 35-44: 15%, 45+: 10%)
- **Best Answer:** PIE CHART or BAR CHART ✓✓
- **Why Pie:** Parts of whole (adds to 100%), limited categories (4), shows proportion
- **Why Bar:** Easier to compare exact percentages, always works
- **Both acceptable!** Choose based on emphasis:
  - Pie if emphasizing "whole audience breakdown"
  - Bar if emphasizing "which age group is largest"
- **Wrong:** Line chart (not a trend)
- **Wrong:** Scatter plot (not correlation)

**What it shows:** Age demographics
**Example insight:** "75% of audience under 35"

---

**Scenario 4:** Determine if more posts = more engagement
- **Best Answer:** SCATTER PLOT ✓
- **Why:** Looking for correlation between two continuous variables
- **X-axis:** Number of posts per week
- **Y-axis:** Average engagement rate
- **Each dot:** One time period (week/month)
- **Trendline:** Shows if correlation exists
- **Wrong:** Any other chart type (none show correlation)

**What it shows:** Relationship (or lack of) between posting frequency and engagement
**Example insight:** "No clear correlation - quality > quantity" OR "Positive correlation up to 7 posts/week, then diminishes"

---

**Scenario 5:** Find best day and time to post (engagement by day of week × hour of day)
- **Best Answer:** HEATMAP ✓
- **Why:** Two dimensions (7 days × 24 hours = 168 cells), color shows engagement level
- **Rows:** Days of week (Mon-Sun)
- **Columns:** Hours (12am-11pm)
- **Color:** Red (high engagement) → Yellow → Green (low engagement)
- **Alternative:** Pivot table with conditional formatting
- **Wrong:** Line chart (can't show two dimensions effectively)
- **Wrong:** Bar chart (would need 168 bars)

**What it shows:** Engagement patterns across week
**Example insight:** "Peak engagement: Tues/Wed 7-9 PM, low on Sunday mornings"

---

**Scenario 6:** Show how total revenue from social campaigns has grown each quarter for 2 years
- **Best Answer:** LINE CHART or AREA CHART ✓✓
- **Why Line:** Shows trend clearly, simple
- **Why Area:** Shows cumulative feel, visual impact of growth
- **Both acceptable!** Line is more common, area has more visual "weight"
- **Wrong:** Bar chart (works but loses trend feel)
- **Wrong:** Pie chart (not parts of a whole)

**What it shows:** Revenue trend over time
**Example insight:** "Revenue grew 150% year-over-year"

---

**Scenario 7:** Compare performance of 5 recent posts (which got most likes)
- **Best Answer:** BAR CHART ✓
- **Why:** Comparing discrete items (5 posts), easy to see winner
- **Horizontal or vertical both work**
- **Enhancement:** Include thumbnail images of posts
- **Alternative:** Column chart with images
- **Wrong:** Line chart (posts aren't sequential time series)
- **Wrong:** Pie chart (not parts of whole)

**What it shows:** Which posts performed best
**Example insight:** "Post #3 (recipe video) outperformed 2:1"

---

**Scenario 8:** Display traffic sources to website (35% Instagram, 25% Facebook, 20% Google, 15% Twitter, 5% Other)
- **Best Answer:** PIE CHART or BAR CHART ✓✓
- **Why Pie:** Classic use case! Percentages add to 100%, limited categories (5), shows proportion
- **Why Bar:** Also great, easier to read exact values
- **Both acceptable!** This is one scenario where pie is genuinely good
- **Wrong:** Line chart (not time series)

**What it shows:** Breakdown of traffic sources
**Example insight:** "Social media (Instagram + Facebook) drive 60% of traffic"

---

**Scenario 9:** Track daily active users on platform over past 90 days
- **Best Answer:** LINE CHART ✓
- **Why:** Daily time series, looking for trends/patterns
- **90 points is manageable** for line chart
- **Alternative:** Area chart (shows volume)
- **Wrong:** Bar chart (90 bars would be cluttered)
- **Wrong:** Scatter plot (time series, not correlation)

**What it shows:** User activity trends
**Example insight:** "Steady growth with weekend dips"

---

**Scenario 10:** See if accounts with more followers also get more likes per post
- **Best Answer:** SCATTER PLOT ✓
- **Why:** Correlation between two variables (followers vs. likes)
- **X-axis:** Follower count
- **Y-axis:** Average likes per post
- **Each dot:** One account
- **Trendline:** Shows correlation strength
- **Wrong:** Any other chart (none show correlation clearly)

**What it shows:** Relationship between audience size and engagement
**Example insight:** "Positive correlation but many outliers - some small accounts punch above weight"

### Quick Reference: Chart Decision Tree

```
START HERE ↓

Do you have TIME SERIES data (dates)?
  ├─ YES → LINE CHART or AREA CHART
  └─ NO → Continue ↓

Are you COMPARING CATEGORIES?
  ├─ YES → BAR CHART
  └─ NO → Continue ↓

Do percentages ADD TO 100% (parts of whole)?
  ├─ YES → PIE CHART (if 3-5 categories) or BAR CHART
  └─ NO → Continue ↓

Looking for CORRELATION between two variables?
  ├─ YES → SCATTER PLOT
  └─ NO → Continue ↓

Do you have TWO DIMENSIONS (like day × hour)?
  ├─ YES → HEATMAP
  └─ NO → Consult instructor!
```

### Formula Reference Sheet

**Key Formulas Used in Dashboard:**

**Total Followers** (Cell B3):
```
=MAX(Data!B:B)
Explanation: Gets most recent (highest) follower count from data column
```

**Engagement Rate** (Cell E3):
```
=AVERAGE(Data!G:G)
OR
=Calculations!B5
Explanation: Average engagement rate across all posts
```

**Format as percentage:** Right-click → Format Cells → Percentage, 1 decimal place

**Total Reach** (Cell H3):
```
=SUM(Data!E:E)
Explanation: Adds up reach from all posts
```

**Posts This Month** (Cell K3):
```
=COUNTA(Data!A:A)-1
Explanation: Counts non-empty cells in date column, minus header
```

**Growth Rate** (if calculating):
```
=(Latest Value - Previous Value) / Previous Value * 100
Example: =(25842 - 24500) / 24500 * 100 = 5.5%
```

**Average Engagement by Content Type** (for comparison chart):
```
=AVERAGEIF(Data!D:D,"Reel",Data!G:G)
Explanation: Average engagement for posts where content type = "Reel"
Repeat for Photo, Video, Carousel
```

### Conditional Formatting Rules

**For Data Sheet Engagement Column:**

**Option 1: Color Scale**
- Select range: G2:G31
- Conditional Formatting → Color Scales
- Choose: Green (high) - Yellow (mid) - Red (low)

**Option 2: Rule-Based**
- Rule 1: If >= 7% → Green fill
- Rule 2: If >= 5% AND < 7% → Yellow fill
- Rule 3: If < 5% → Red fill

**How to create rules:**
1. Select G2:G31
2. Conditional Formatting → New Rule
3. "Format cells based on their value"
4. Type: 2-Color Scale or 3-Color Scale
5. Set thresholds and colors

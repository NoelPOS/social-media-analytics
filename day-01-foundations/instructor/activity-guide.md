## Activity 1: Icebreaker

### 🎯 Objective
Build rapport, create comfortable learning environment, assess student backgrounds

### ⏱️ Duration
10 minutes total
- 3 minutes: Pair sharing
- 5 minutes: Whole group share-outs
- 2 minutes: Transition

### 👥 Format
Pairs → Whole class

### 📝 Instructions

**Step 1: Set Up (1 min)**
1. Have students turn to person next to them
2. If odd number, create one group of three
3. Display discussion prompts on screen

**Step 2: Pair Sharing (3 min)**
Tell students to introduce themselves and share:
- Name and major/year
- Favorite social media platform and why
- One thing they hope to learn from this course

Set timer visible to class

**Step 3: Whole Group Share (5 min)**
- Call on 4-5 students to briefly share with class
- Focus on the "one thing they want to learn"
- Note common themes on board

Questions to ask:
- "Who's interested in becoming a social media manager?"
- "Anyone here managing social accounts already?"
- "Who loves data and analytics?"

**Step 4: Transition (1 min)**
- Acknowledge common interests
- "Great! We'll cover all of those topics"
- Connect interests to day's agenda

### 🎯 Learning Check
- Students appear comfortable speaking
- You've learned some names
- You understand class interests/goals

### 🔧 Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Students shy/reluctant to share
**Solution:** Share your own answers first as model

**Issue:** One student dominating
**Solution:** "Great! Let's hear from someone else now"

**Issue:** Running long
**Solution:** Cut to 3 share-outs instead of 5

### 📊 Success Indicators
- ✅ Students are talking to each other
- ✅ Energy level is positive
- ✅ You've noted 3-5 key student interests

## Activity 2: Case Study Discussions

### 🎯 Objective
Understand real-world applications of social media analytics

### ⏱️ Duration
20 minutes total
- 15 minutes: Present 3 case studies (5 min each)
- 5 minutes: Discussion

### 👥 Format
Whole class presentation → Small group discussion

### 📝 Instructions

**Step 1: Present Case Study #1 - Local Bakery (5 min)**

Tell the story:
```
"Meet Sarah, owner of a small neighborhood bakery. 
After 2 years on Instagram, she had only 500 followers 
and sales weren't growing.

One day, she checked her Instagram Insights and 
discovered something interesting: Her behind-the-scenes 
videos got 3x more engagement than photos of finished 
products.

She started a daily 'Morning Baking' series showing 
the process. She also noticed her audience was most 
active at 6 AM, so she posted then.

Results after 6 months:
- 500 → 15,000 followers
- Website traffic up 300%
- Lines out the door on weekends
- Featured in local news
"
```

**Show:**
- Before/after Instagram profiles (if available)
- Insights screenshots showing engagement rates
- Growth chart

**Ask:**
- "What was the key insight?"
- "What action did she take?"

**Step 2: Present Case Study #2 - Animal Shelter (5 min)**

Tell the story:
```
"Happy Paws Animal Shelter was struggling with 
fundraising. Traditional methods weren't working.

They analyzed their Facebook data and found that 
video posts received 5x more shares and 3x more 
donations than photo posts.

They started creating weekly 'Adoption Success Story' 
videos - 1-minute clips of adopted pets in their new homes.

Results:
- Video views increased 800%
- Donation conversions up 150%
- Adoptions increased 50%
- Became regional model for shelter marketing
"
```

**Show:**
- Video performance metrics
- Donation tracking data
- Before/after comparison

**Ask:**
- "Why did videos perform better?"
- "What emotions do videos tap into?"

**Step 3: Present Case Study #3 - Fitness Influencer (5 min)**

Tell the story:
```
"Jake was a fitness trainer posting workout videos 
daily at 6 AM (when he worked out). He had 10,000 
followers but engagement was flat.

He checked Instagram Insights and discovered his 
audience was most active between 7-9 PM, not 6 AM! 
They were busy people who worked out after work.

He shifted his posting schedule to 7:30 PM and started 
using Stories more actively.

Results:
- Engagement rate: 3% → 6% (doubled!)
- 10K → 50K followers in 4 months
- First brand sponsorship deal
- Quit day job to go full-time
"
```

**Show:**
- Audience activity chart
- Engagement rate comparison
- Growth trajectory

**Ask:**
- "What was Jake's mistake?"
- "Why does timing matter so much?"

**Step 4: Group Discussion (5 min)**

Put students in groups of 3-4. Give discussion prompts:

1. "What pattern do you see across all three examples?"
2. "How did analytics lead to specific actions?"
3. "What surprises you about these outcomes?"
4. "How might you apply these lessons?"

Circulate and listen to discussions.

**Step 5: Debrief (3 min)**

Call on 2-3 groups to share insights. Synthesize:

**Key Takeaways:**
- Data reveals what's actually working (not what you think)
- Small changes can have big impacts
- Timing matters as much as content
- Every audience is unique
- Testing and iterating is essential

### 🎯 Learning Check
Students can articulate:
- How analytics led to insights
- Why data-driven decisions matter
- That small changes can scale

### 🔧 Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Students seem skeptical of results
**Solution:** Show actual screenshots, emphasize these are real examples

**Issue:** Discussion not happening
**Solution:** Call on specific students, seed discussion with your own observation

**Issue:** Running short on time
**Solution:** Present 2 case studies instead of 3

### 📊 Success Indicators
- ✅ Students see value of analytics
- ✅ Students connecting to own experiences
- ✅ Engaged discussion happening

## Activity 3: Metric Categorization Game

### 🎯 Objective
Distinguish between vanity and actionable metrics

### ⏱️ Duration
10-12 minutes

### 👥 Format
Whole class interactive voting

### 📝 Instructions

**Step 1: Explain Rules (2 min)**

Say to class:
```
"I'm going to show you different metrics. For each one, 
you'll decide: Is it a VANITY metric or an ACTIONABLE metric?

Remember:
- VANITY = Looks impressive but doesn't drive decisions
- ACTIONABLE = Tied to goals, leads to specific actions

Use your fingers:
V = Make a V shape with two fingers
A = Make an A shape with fingers (point up like triangle)

Ready? Here we go!"
```

**Step 2: Show Metrics One at a Time**

Present each metric on screen. Give students 5 seconds to vote.

**Metric 1: "10,000 followers"**
- **Answer: VANITY** (by itself)
- **Discussion:** "It's impressive, but what does it tell us? Are they engaged? Growing? Converting?"
- **Exception:** "If your goal IS to grow followers, then it becomes actionable when tracked over time"

**Metric 2: "5% conversion rate"**
- **Answer: ACTIONABLE**
- **Discussion:** "Directly measures business goal. You can optimize for this."

**Metric 3: "1 million impressions"**
- **Answer: VANITY** (usually)
- **Discussion:** "Big number! But did anyone take action? Were they the right people?"

**Metric 4: "8% engagement rate"**
- **Answer: ACTIONABLE**
- **Discussion:** "Shows how content resonates. Can compare across posts to find patterns."

**Metric 5: "Featured in top posts hashtag"**
- **Answer: VANITY**
- **Discussion:** "Feels good! But doesn't tell us impact on business goals."

**Metric 6: "25% click-through rate on email campaign"**
- **Answer: ACTIONABLE**
- **Discussion:** "Measures specific desired action. Can optimize."

**Metric 7: "Posted 100 times this month"**
- **Answer: VANITY**
- **Discussion:** "Activity doesn't equal effectiveness. Quality over quantity!"

**Metric 8: "Average watch time: 2 minutes (target: 1 minute)"**
- **Answer: ACTIONABLE**
- **Discussion:** "Exceeding goal! Content is engaging. Can double down on this format."

**Metric 9: "Verified badge on profile"**
- **Answer: VANITY**
- **Discussion:** "Credibility boost, but not a performance metric."

**Metric 10: "30% reduction in cost per acquisition"**
- **Answer: ACTIONABLE**
- **Discussion:** "Direct business impact. Shows efficiency improvement."

**Step 3: Key Takeaway (2 min)**

Summarize:
```
"Notice the pattern? Actionable metrics:
✓ Tie to specific goals
✓ Suggest specific actions
✓ Measure outcomes, not just activity

Vanity metrics:
✗ Look impressive but lack context
✗ Don't suggest what to do next
✗ Often measure volume, not value

HOWEVER - Context matters! A metric can be vanity 
in one context and actionable in another."
```

**Step 4: Challenge Question (2 min)**

Final question for class:
"Your CEO says: 'I want 100,000 followers!'
Is that vanity or actionable?"

**Answer:** "It depends! Ask follow-up questions:
- Why do we want 100K followers?
- What will they help us achieve?
- What's the business goal behind this?"

### 🎯 Learning Check
Students can:
- Differentiate vanity from actionable
- Explain their reasoning
- Understand context matters

### 🔧 Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Students confused about a metric
**Solution:** Discuss as group, explain both perspectives

**Issue:** Everyone voting same thing without thinking
**Solution:** Speed up or challenge with harder examples

**Issue:** Debate getting too long
**Solution:** "Both valid! Let's move forward."

### 📊 Success Indicators
- ✅ Students actively voting
- ✅ Thoughtful debate on borderline cases
- ✅ Understanding that context matters

## Activity 4: Engagement Rate Calculations

### 🎯 Objective
Practice calculating engagement rate from real data

### ⏱️ Duration
8-10 minutes

### 👥 Format
Individual → Whole class

### 📝 Instructions

**Step 1: Review Formula (2 min)**

Write on board:
```
Engagement Rate = (Total Engagements / Reach) × 100

Where:
Total Engagements = Likes + Comments + Shares
```

**Work through example together:**
```
Post has:
- 50 likes
- 10 comments
- 5 shares
- Reached 1,000 people

Step 1: Add engagements
50 + 10 + 5 = 65

Step 2: Divide by reach
65 / 1,000 = 0.065

Step 3: Multiply by 100
0.065 × 100 = 6.5%

Answer: 6.5% engagement rate
```

**Step 2: Practice Problem (3 min)**

Display problem:
```
Calculate engagement rate:
- 120 likes
- 25 comments
- 15 shares
- 2,500 reach
```

Students calculate individually. Set timer for 2 minutes.

**Step 3: Review Answer (2 min)**

Ask for volunteers to share answer.

**Correct answer: 6.4%**

Walk through on board:
```
120 + 25 + 15 = 160
160 / 2,500 = 0.064
0.064 × 100 = 6.4%
```

**Step 4: Interpretation (2 min)**

Ask class:
- "Is 6.4% good or bad?"
- "What's the average?" (Answer: 1-5% is typical)
- "So this is excellent!"

**Context questions:**
- "What might cause high engagement?"
- "What might cause low engagement?"

Possible answers:
- Content quality
- Posting time
- Audience size/quality
- Topic relevance
- Platform differences

**Step 5: Additional Practice (Optional)**

If time permits, give 2 more problems:

**Problem 2:**
- 800 likes, 150 comments, 50 shares
- 25,000 reach
- **Answer: 4%**

**Problem 3:**
- 15 likes, 3 comments, 1 share
- 500 reach
- **Answer: 3.8%**

### 🎯 Learning Check
Students can:
- Calculate engagement rate accurately
- Interpret what the number means
- Compare rates to benchmarks

### 🔧 Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Students struggling with math
**Solution:** Walk through step-by-step again, allow calculators

**Issue:** Students finish at different rates
**Solution:** Give faster students additional problems

**Issue:** Confusion about formula
**Solution:** Show visual diagram of formula

### 📊 Success Indicators
- ✅ Most students get correct answer
- ✅ Students using formula correctly
- ✅ Students understand what number means

### 💡 Extensions

**Advanced students:**
- Calculate weighted engagement (comments worth 2x likes)
- Compare engagement rates across platforms
- Calculate what engagement rate needs to be to hit goal

## Activity 5: Platform Analytics Tour

### 🎯 Objective
Navigate native analytics tools on major platforms

### ⏱️ Duration
45 minutes total
- 20 min: Facebook/Meta Business Suite
- 20 min: Instagram Insights
- 5 min: Quick tour of Twitter & LinkedIn

### 👥 Format
Instructor-led demo → Student follow-along

### 📝 Instructions

### Part A: Facebook Business Suite (20 min)

**Step 1: Access (3 min)**

**Instructor Demo:**
1. Navigate to business.facebook.com
2. Log in to demo account
3. Show how to access Insights

**Student Action:**
- Students access their own Facebook Business Suite
- Or watch demo if no business page

**Troubleshooting:**
- Students without business pages can observe
- Or quickly convert personal page

**Step 2: Overview Dashboard (4 min)**

**Show and Explain:**
1. **Overview tab** - High-level metrics
   - Page likes/followers
   - Post reach
   - Page views
   - Actions on page

2. **Date range selector**
   - Last 7 days, 28 days, custom
   - Compare periods

3. **Key metrics cards**
   - Page likes
   - Post reach
   - Engagement
   - Actions

**Student Action:**
- Navigate to Overview on their account
- Change date range
- Note key metrics

**Step 3: Content Tab (5 min)**

**Show and Explain:**
1. **Posts performance**
   - Recent posts list
   - Reach, engagement per post
   - Sort by metric

2. **Top posts**
   - Best performing content
   - What makes them successful?

3. **Post types**
   - Photo vs. video vs. link performance
   - Stories analytics

**Student Action:**
- Find their top performing post
- Identify patterns

**Key Questions:**
- "Which post type performs best for you?"
- "What day/time worked best?"

**Step 4: Audience Tab (4 min)**

**Show and Explain:**
1. **Demographics**
   - Age and gender breakdown
   - Location (cities, countries)
   - Language

2. **When fans are online**
   - Days of week
   - Time of day
   - Heatmap visualization

3. **Fan vs. reached audience**
   - Difference between followers and who you actually reach

**Student Action:**
- Find their peak activity time
- Note top demographic

**Step 5: Export Feature (4 min)**

**Show and Explain:**
1. Click "Export Data" button
2. Select date range
3. Choose data type (Page data, Post data, Video data)
4. Choose format (Excel or CSV)
5. Export and download

**Student Action:**
- Export last 28 days of data
- Open file to see structure

### Part B: Instagram Insights (20 min)

**Step 1: Access (3 min)**

**Two methods:**

**Mobile:**
1. Go to profile
2. Tap menu (three lines)
3. Tap "Insights"

**Desktop:**
1. Go to business.facebook.com
2. Select Instagram account
3. View Insights

**Student Action:**
- Access Insights on mobile or desktop
- Note: Need Professional account

**Step 2: Account Insights (5 min)**

**Show and Explain:**
1. **Overview**
   - Accounts reached
   - Accounts engaged
   - Total followers

2. **Content you shared**
   - Posts
   - Stories
   - Reels
   - Videos
   - Live

3. **Audience**
   - Growth
   - Demographics
   - Active times

**Navigate through each tab**

**Student Action:**
- Explore each section
- Note 3 interesting insights

**Step 3: Content Performance (5 min)**

**Show and Explain:**
1. **Feed posts**
   - Reach, likes, comments, shares, saves
   - Profile visits from post
   - SAVES are crucial!

2. **Stories**
   - Impressions
   - Reach
   - Exits, next story, back
   - Replies
   - Completion rate

3. **Reels**
   - Plays
   - Accounts reached
   - Likes, comments, shares, saves
   - Watch time

**Student Action:**
- Find top performing feed post
- Check story completion rate
- Identify best reel (if any)

**Key Insight:** "Saves signal high value to algorithm!"

**Step 4: Audience Insights (4 min)**

**Show and Explain:**
1. **Follower growth**
   - Net gain/loss
   - Chart over time

2. **Demographics**
   - Age range
   - Gender
   - Top locations (cities, countries)

3. **Most active times**
   - Days of week
   - Hours of day
   - Specific hours heat map

**Student Action:**
- Screenshot their best posting time
- Note top 3 locations

**Step 5: Professional Dashboard (3 min)**

**Show:**
- Account type and category
- Profile views
- Website taps
- Email/phone taps
- Quick stats summary

### Part C: Quick Tours (5 min)

**Twitter Analytics (2 min)**

Quick demo:
1. Navigate to analytics.twitter.com
2. Show dashboard
3. Point out tweet activity
4. Note impressions and engagements

**LinkedIn Analytics (2 min)**

Quick demo:
1. Go to company/profile page
2. Click "Analytics"
3. Show visitors, updates, followers tabs
4. Note demographic breakdown

**Wrap-up (1 min)**
"Each platform has similar but unique metrics. Practice exploring!"

### 🎯 Learning Check
Students can:
- Access analytics on each platform
- Navigate to key sections
- Export data

### 🔧 Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Student doesn't have business account
**Solution:** Watch demo, pair with student who has one

**Issue:** Not enough data showing
**Solution:** Use instructor demo account as example

**Issue:** Technical difficulties
**Solution:** Have screenshots as backup

**Issue:** Wide range of experience levels
**Solution:** Pair experienced with beginners

### 📊 Success Indicators
- ✅ Students successfully accessing Insights
- ✅ Students finding their top metrics
- ✅ Students understanding what each metric means
- ✅ Students comfortable navigating tools

## Activity 6: Metric Scavenger Hunt

### 🎯 Objective
Apply knowledge to find and record real metrics

### ⏱️ Duration
45 minutes total
- 5 min: Setup and team formation
- 30 min: Scavenger hunt
- 10 min: Debrief and winner announcement

### 👥 Format
Teams of 3-4 students

### 📝 Instructions

**Step 1: Setup (5 min)**

**Form Teams:**
1. Divide class into teams of 3-4
2. Mix experience levels
3. Give each team:
   - Scavenger hunt worksheet
   - List of accounts to analyze
   - Pen/paper or digital document

**Explain Rules:**
```
"You'll work as a team to find specific metrics from 
real social media accounts. 

RULES:
1. Work together - divide and conquer!
2. Must find ACTUAL data (no guessing)
3. Screenshot or document your source
4. First team with ALL correct answers wins

PRIZE: [Small prize - candy, extra credit, bragging rights]

TIME: 30 minutes

Any questions? Go!"
```

**Step 2: The Hunt Begins (30 min)**

**Worksheet Contents:**

Provide list of 15-20 metrics to find across platforms.

**Example Tasks:**

**FACEBOOK SECTION:**
1. Find Brand X's page: How many page likes?
2. What was their reach last week?
3. Find their most recent post: What's the engagement rate?
4. What percentage of their audience is female?
5. What day are their fans most active?

**INSTAGRAM SECTION:**
6. Find Influencer Y's profile: How many followers?
7. Find their recent post: Calculate engagement rate from top 5 posts
8. How many profile visits this week?
9. What time of day are followers most active?
10. Find a post with over 1,000 saves

**TWITTER SECTION:**
11. Find Company Z's Twitter: How many followers?
12. Find a recent tweet: What's the impression count?
13. What's their average engagement rate on last 5 tweets?
14. Find their most retweeted tweet this month
15. How many replies on their latest tweet?

**LINKEDIN SECTION:**
16. Find Professional Page W: How many followers?
17. What's their engagement rate on recent posts?
18. What job functions follow them most?
19. Find their top performing post this month
20. What industry comprises most of their audience?

**BONUS CHALLENGES:** (Extra points)
- Find a brand with over 10% engagement rate
- Find an example of excellent crisis response
- Find a brand that posts at optimal times

**Instructor Role During Hunt:**
- Circulate to help teams
- Answer clarifying questions
- Provide hints if team is stuck
- Monitor time - give updates at 15 min and 5 min remaining
- Check submissions as teams finish

**Step 3: Verification (5 min)**

As teams submit:
1. Quickly verify major answers
2. Check screenshots/sources
3. Note time of completion
4. Give feedback on accuracy

**Step 4: Debrief (10 min)**

Once time is up or winner determined:

**Announce Winner:**
"Team [X] completed first with [Y]% accuracy! Great job!"

**Discussion Questions:**

1. **"What was the easiest metric to find?"**
   - Expected: Follower counts, likes
   - Why: Public and prominently displayed

2. **"What was the hardest?"**
   - Expected: Engagement rates (require calculation), audience demographics (need account access)
   - Why: Requires more digging or access

3. **"What surprised you?"**
   - Listen to various responses
   - Common: How much data is publicly available, or how hard some things are to find

4. **"What patterns did you notice?"**
   - Successful brands post consistently
   - High engagement doesn't always correlate with high followers
   - Timing matters
   - Video often outperforms photos

5. **"How would you use this in a real job?"**
   - Competitive analysis
   - Benchmarking
   - Identifying best practices
   - Learning from successful accounts

**Key Takeaways:**
```
✅ Lots of data is publicly available
✅ Some metrics require account access
✅ Practice makes navigation faster
✅ Analytics skills are practical and valuable
✅ Every brand has different strategies
```

### 🎯 Learning Check
Students demonstrated:
- Ability to navigate platforms
- Find specific metrics
- Calculate derived metrics (like engagement rate)
- Work collaboratively
- Apply knowledge under time pressure

### 🔧 Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Team finishing way ahead of others
**Solution:** Give bonus challenges, have them verify other teams

**Issue:** Team totally stuck
**Solution:** Provide hints, redirect to right section

**Issue:** Technical issues (accounts locked, etc.)
**Solution:** Provide alternative accounts to analyze

**Issue:** Dispute about correct answer
**Solution:** Show official source, explain discrepancies

**Issue:** Teams not working together
**Solution:** Require all members to contribute, rotate roles

### 📊 Success Indicators
- ✅ All teams actively engaged
- ✅ Students using proper tools/methods
- ✅ Teams finding metrics successfully
- ✅ Collaborative problem-solving happening
- ✅ Students excited/competitive

### 💡 Teaching Tips

**Make it Fun:**
- Play energetic music during hunt
- Give countdown warnings dramatically
- Celebrate discoveries
- Offer small prizes

**Maximize Learning:**
- Mix skill levels in teams
- Rotate who does what
- Require documentation/screenshots
- Debrief thoroughly

**Adapt for Context:**
- Shorter hunt if running behind (20 min)
- Longer if students very engaged (40 min)
- Easier metrics for beginners
- Harder metrics for advanced students

### 📋 Materials Needed

**Prepare in Advance:**
- [ ] Printed or digital worksheet
- [ ] List of accounts to analyze (accessible, interesting brands)
- [ ] Answer key for verification
- [ ] Prize(s) for winner
- [ ] Timer visible to class
- [ ] Backup accounts in case some are unavailable

**Suggested Accounts to Use:**
- Nike (Facebook, Instagram, Twitter)
- Starbucks (Facebook, Instagram)
- National Geographic (Instagram)
- NASA (Twitter, Facebook)
- Local university accounts
- Regional brands students recognize

**Choose accounts that:**
- Are public/accessible
- Post regularly
- Have varied content
- Are relevant to student interests
- Have accessible metrics

## 🎯 Summary for Instructors

### Activity Sequence Recap

| Time | Activity | Format | Energy Level |
|------|----------|--------|--------------|
| 10:00-10:10 | Icebreaker | Pairs | ⚡ Warm-up |
| 10:15-10:35 | Case Studies | Whole class | 📚 Lecture |
| 11:15-11:27 | Metric Categorization | Interactive | ⚡⚡ High energy |
| 11:27-11:35 | Calculations | Individual | 🧮 Focus |
| 1:30-2:15 | Platform Tour | Demo | 💻 Technical |
| 3:15-4:00 | Scavenger Hunt | Teams | ⚡⚡⚡ Very high |

### Energy Management

**High Energy Activities:**
- Metric Categorization (game-like)
- Scavenger Hunt (competitive)
- Icebreaker (social)

**Lower Energy Activities:**
- Calculations (focus required)
- Platform tour (technical learning)
- Case studies (listening)

**Balance:** Alternate high and low energy activities

### Adaptation Guide

**For Larger Classes (30+ students):**
- Use microphone for activities
- Project timer on screen
- Create more teams (max 4 per team)
- Have TAs help during scavenger hunt

**For Smaller Classes (<15 students):**
- More personalized attention
- Can go deeper in discussions
- Longer Q&A periods
- Individual work instead of teams

**For Online Delivery:**
- Use breakout rooms for group work
- Screen share for demos
- Use polls for voting activities
- Digital collaboration docs for scavenger hunt

**For Mixed Abilities:**
- Pair experienced with beginners
- Provide optional advanced challenges
- Have basics and stretch goals
- Offer one-on-one help during activities

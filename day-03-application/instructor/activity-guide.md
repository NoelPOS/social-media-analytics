## 🎯 ACTIVITY 1: Competitive Audit Workshop

**Time:** 20 minutes (10 min work + 10 min discussion)  
**Format:** Individual work, then pair-share  
**Timing:** 10:25-10:35 AM (after competitive analysis lecture)

### Objectives

By the end of this activity, students will:
- Calculate engagement rates for multiple competitors
- Create a competitive benchmarking matrix
- Calculate share of voice (SOV)
- Identify competitive strengths and opportunities

### Materials Needed

**Per Student:**
- Competitive analysis dataset (printed or digital)
- Competitive matrix template
- Calculator or Excel
- Pen/pencil

**Dataset Contents:**
Five fitness brands with:
- Follower counts
- Average likes per post
- Average comments per post
- Posts per week
- Monthly mentions (for SOV)

### Setup Instructions

**Before Activity:**
1. Print competitive analysis dataset (1 per student) OR share digitally
2. Print competitive matrix template (1 per student)
3. Ensure calculators available or Excel accessible
4. Write SOV formula on board for reference

**Room Setup:**
- Students can work at their seats
- Timer visible to all

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Instructor Script:**

"You'll receive a dataset with 5 competitors in the fitness industry: FitLife Gym, PowerHouse Fitness, Zen Yoga Studio, CrossFit Central, and ActiveLife Wellness.

Your tasks:

**1. Calculate Engagement Rate for each brand (5 min)**
- Formula: (Likes + Comments) / Followers × 100
- Round to one decimal place

**2. Complete the competitive matrix (3 min)**
- Fill in all metrics for each brand
- Mark the highest performer in each category with a ✓

**3. Calculate Share of Voice (2 min)**
- Formula: (Brand Mentions / Total Industry Mentions) × 100
- Total all five brands' mentions first

**4. Identify insights (2 min)**
- Which brand is winning overall?
- What are the key competitive dynamics?
- Where are the opportunities?

You have 10 minutes. Begin!"

**During Activity:**
- Set timer for 10 minutes
- Circulate to check progress
- Provide calculation hints if needed
- Note interesting findings for discussion

**After Individual Work (5 min pair-share):**

"Turn to someone near you. Compare your findings:
- Did you get the same engagement rates?
- What insights did you identify?
- Any surprises in the data?

2-3 minutes to discuss."

**Class Discussion (5 min):**

"Let's discuss as a group. Who had the highest engagement rate?"

(Take answers, confirm correct: Zen Yoga Studio at 8.2%)

"Why might a smaller brand like Zen Yoga have higher engagement than bigger brands?"

(Expected answers: niche focus, more engaged community, quality over quantity)

"What about share of voice—who's winning the conversation?"

(Correct: PowerHouse Fitness at 32%)

"If you were managing FitLife Gym, what would be your strategy based on this data?"

(Discussion points: they have good size but low engagement, need to improve content quality, SOV is moderate—room for growth)

### Solutions

**Engagement Rates:**
- FitLife Gym: (380 + 22) / 45,000 × 100 = **0.89%**
- PowerHouse Fitness: (520 + 28) / 62,000 × 100 = **0.88%**
- Zen Yoga Studio: (680 + 48) / 18,000 × 100 = **4.04%** ✓ HIGHEST
- CrossFit Central: (450 + 35) / 51,000 × 100 = **0.95%**
- ActiveLife Wellness: (285 + 18) / 28,000 × 100 = **1.08%**

**Total Industry Mentions:** 5,200 + 8,100 + 3,400 + 6,800 + 2,500 = 26,000

**Share of Voice:**
- FitLife Gym: 5,200 / 26,000 × 100 = **20.0%**
- PowerHouse Fitness: 8,100 / 26,000 × 100 = **31.2%** ✓ HIGHEST
- Zen Yoga Studio: 3,400 / 26,000 × 100 = **13.1%**
- CrossFit Central: 6,800 / 26,000 × 100 = **26.2%**
- ActiveLife Wellness: 2,500 / 26,000 × 100 = **9.6%**

**Completed Matrix:**

| Metric | FitLife | PowerHouse | Zen Yoga | CrossFit | ActiveLife |
|--------|---------|------------|----------|----------|-----------|
| Followers | 45K | 62K ✓ | 18K | 51K | 28K |
| Engagement Rate | 0.89% | 0.88% | 4.04% ✓ | 0.95% | 1.08% |
| Posts/Week | 7 | 10 ✓ | 5 | 8 | 6 |
| SOV | 20.0% | 31.2% ✓ | 13.1% | 26.2% | 9.6% |

**Key Insights:**
- PowerHouse Fitness dominates size (followers, SOV, posting frequency)
- Zen Yoga Studio has 4× higher engagement despite smallest size (quality over quantity)
- FitLife Gym is middle-of-the-pack—decent size, but low engagement (opportunity to improve content)
- ActiveLife Wellness is smallest and lowest SOV (growth opportunity or niche focus?)

### Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Students confused about engagement rate formula  
**Solution:** Write it clearly on board: (Likes + Comments) / Followers × 100

**Issue:** Calculation errors  
**Solution:** Check if they're dividing correctly, using commas in numbers

**Issue:** Don't understand SOV  
**Solution:** Explain as "pie chart of conversation"—how big is your slice?

**Issue:** Finished early  
**Solution:** Ask them to write 3 specific recommendations for one brand

**Issue:** Can't see insights  
**Solution:** Prompt: "Who's winning? Who's losing? Why? What would you do?"

### Extension Activity

For advanced students or extra time:
"Calculate engagement per 1,000 followers for each brand. Formula: Total Engagements / Followers × 1,000"

**Solutions:**

- PowerHouse: (548 / 62,000) × 1,000 = 8.8 per 1K
- Zen Yoga: (728 / 18,000) × 1,000 = 40.4 per 1K ✓
- CrossFit: (485 / 51,000) × 1,000 = 9.5 per 1K
- ActiveLife: (303 / 28,000) × 1,000 = 10.8 per 1K

## 🔗 ACTIVITY 2: Campaign Tracking Setup

**Time:** 15 minutes (10 min work + 5 min review)  
**Format:** Individual practice  
**Timing:** 11:15-11:25 AM (after campaign tracking lecture)

### Objectives

By the end of this activity, students will:
- Create properly formatted UTM parameters
- Build 5 different tracked URLs
- Understand UTM naming conventions
- Apply consistent formatting rules

### Materials Needed

**Per Student:**
- Campaign tracking worksheet
- UTM parameter reference sheet
- Access to Google Campaign URL Builder (optional)

**Campaign Scenario:**
EcoBottle Reusable Water Bottle - Earth Day Promotion

### Setup Instructions

**Before Activity:**
1. Print campaign tracking worksheet (1 per student)
2. Write UTM structure on board as reference
3. Optional: Share Google Campaign URL Builder link
4. Prepare example on board

**Example on Board:**
```
Base URL: www.ecobottle.com/earthday

Campaign: earth_day_2026
Source: instagram
Medium: social
Content: story_ad_1

Full URL: www.ecobottle.com/earthday?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=earth_day_2026&utm_content=story_ad_1
```

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Instructor Script:**

"You're launching a campaign for EcoBottle Reusable Water Bottle. It's Earth Day week, and you're running promotional content across multiple platforms.

**Your Campaign Details:**
- Campaign name: Earth Day Promotion
- Base URL: www.ecobottle.com/earthday
- Platforms: Instagram, Facebook, Twitter, Email
- Content types: Story ads, feed posts, carousel ads, newsletter

**Your Task: Create 5 UTM-tagged URLs for:**

1. Instagram story ad (paid)
2. Instagram organic feed post
3. Facebook carousel ad (paid)
4. Twitter organic post
5. Email newsletter link

**Rules to follow:**
- All lowercase
- Use underscores (not spaces): earth_day_2026
- Be consistent with naming
- Be specific with content parameter

Use the worksheet template. You have 10 minutes!"

**During Activity:**
- Set timer for 10 minutes
- Circulate to check work
- Look for common mistakes:
  - Inconsistent capitalization
  - Spaces instead of underscores
  - Wrong source names (Insta vs instagram)
  - Missing parameters
- Provide guidance, not answers

**Coaching Tips While Circulating:**
- "Should source be 'Instagram' or 'instagram'?" (lowercase!)
- "What's the difference between the Instagram story ad and feed post?" (paid vs organic medium)
- "Is your content parameter specific enough to know which piece of content this is?"

```

### Review (5 min)

**Instructor Script:**

"Let's check one together. Who wants to share their Instagram story ad URL?"

(Take volunteer, write on board, or project if digital)

**Check for:**
- ✅ utm_source=instagram (all lowercase, spelled out)
- ✅ utm_medium=paid_social or cpc (indicates it's an ad)
- ✅ utm_campaign=earth_day_2026 (consistent across all)
- ✅ utm_content=story_ad_april20 (specific enough to identify)

"Perfect! Notice:
- All lowercase—consistency matters
- Source is platform name: instagram, facebook, twitter
- Medium tells us type: paid_social vs social, or email
- Campaign is same across ALL touchpoints
- Content is specific: story_ad, carousel_ad, tweet, etc.

In Google Analytics, you'll now see exactly which content drives results!"

**Common Questions to Address:**

Q: "Does capitalization really matter?"  
A: "Yes! Analytics sees 'Instagram' and 'instagram' as different sources. Pick one and stick to it."

Q: "Should I use 'paid_social' or 'cpc' for ads?"  
A: "Either works—just be consistent. 'paid_social' is clearer for most people."

Q: "How specific should content be?"  
A: "Specific enough to know what it is when you look at data later. 'post1' is too vague. 'story_ad_april20' is clear."

### Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Using spaces instead of underscores  
**Solution:** Explain spaces break URLs; underscores keep it as one word

**Issue:** Inconsistent source naming (Instagram vs instagram vs IG)  
**Solution:** Emphasize: pick one convention, use everywhere

**Issue:** Same content parameter for different pieces  
**Solution:** Ask: "If both say 'ad', how will you know which ad performed better?"

**Issue:** Forgetting to include base URL  
**Solution:** Remind: start with base, add question mark, then parameters

### Extension Activity

"Now test your URLs! Paste them into a browser and check:
- Do they work? (Should go to the page)
- Are there any errors?

Then, if you have Google Analytics access, check if the parameters would show up in Acquisition → Campaigns."

## 💰 ACTIVITY 3: ROI Calculation Exercise

**Time:** 20 minutes (15 min work + 5 min review morning, complete review after lunch)  
**Format:** Individual work with Excel/calculator  
**Timing:** 11:45-12:00 PM (before lunch)

### Objectives

By the end of this activity, students will:
- Calculate ROI for different campaign scenarios
- Calculate cost per acquisition (CPA)
- Evaluate campaign success
- Make data-driven recommendations

### Materials Needed

**Per Student:**
- ROI calculation scenarios handout (4 scenarios)
- Calculator or Excel
- Formula reference sheet

**Formulas Provided:**
- ROI = (Revenue - Investment) / Investment × 100
- CPA = Total Cost / Number of Conversions

### Setup Instructions

**Before Activity:**
1. Print ROI scenarios handout (1 per student)
2. Ensure calculators available or Excel accessible
3. Write formulas on board for reference
4. Prepare solutions for review after lunch

**Board Setup:**
```
ROI = (Revenue - Investment) / Investment × 100
CPA = Total Cost / Conversions

Interpretation:
ROI > 100% = Making money
ROI = 0% = Break even
ROI < 0% = Losing money
```

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Instructor Script:**

"You'll receive 4 different campaign scenarios. For each campaign:

**1. Calculate ROI**
- Use formula: (Revenue - Investment) / Investment × 100
- Show your work

**2. Calculate CPA**
- Use formula: Total Cost / Number of Conversions
- Show your work

**3. Evaluate: Is this campaign successful?**
- Consider ROI, CPA, campaign goals
- Think about context

**4. Recommend: Scale up, optimize, or shut down?**
- Be specific about why

You have 15 minutes. We'll review the first scenario now, then the rest after lunch. Begin!"

**During Activity:**
- Set timer for 15 minutes
- Circulate to check progress
- Provide calculation help if needed
- Don't give answers, but guide thinking
- Note common errors for group review

**Hints to Provide:**
- "Check your formula—are you dividing by investment?"
- "Is $20 CPA good? Depends on what you're selling!"
- "Think about campaign type—awareness vs conversion"

### The Four Scenarios

```

Goal: Build awareness for new product launch
Investment: $2,000
- Ad spend: $1,500
- Content creation: $500

Direct Results:
- Sales tracked: $1,000 (20 purchases)
- Impressions: 100,000
- New followers: 5,000
- Brand mentions increased 200%

Calculate:
1. ROI = ?
2. CPA = ?
3. Is this successful?
4. Recommendation?

────────────────────────────────────

SCENARIO 2: PRODUCT LAUNCH CAMPAIGN

Goal: Drive sales for new product
Investment: $5,000
- Ad spend: $3,500
- Influencer: $1,000
- Creative: $500

Direct Results:
- Sales tracked: $25,000 (250 purchases)
- Average order value: $100
- Website traffic: 10,000 clicks

Calculate:
1. ROI = ?
2. CPA = ?
3. Is this successful?
4. Recommendation?

────────────────────────────────────

SCENARIO 3: RETARGETING CAMPAIGN

Goal: Convert website visitors who didn't buy
Investment: $1,000
- Retargeting ads: $1,000

Direct Results:
- Sales tracked: $8,000 (80 conversions)
- Average order value: $100
- 5% of ad viewers converted

Calculate:
1. ROI = ?
2. CPA = ?
3. Is this successful?
4. Recommendation?

────────────────────────────────────

SCENARIO 4: FAILED FLASH SALE

Goal: Drive urgent sales with 48-hour sale
Investment: $3,000
- Ad spend: $2,200
- Production: $500
- Tools: $300

Direct Results:
- Sales tracked: $1,200 (20 purchases)
- Average order value: $60
- Many views, few conversions

Calculate:
1. ROI = ?
2. CPA = ?
3. Is this successful?
4. Recommendation?
```

### Solutions

**SCENARIO 1: Brand Awareness**

**Calculations:**
- ROI = ($1,000 - $2,000) / $2,000 × 100 = **-50%**
- CPA = $2,000 / 20 = **$100 per customer**

**Evaluation:**
❓ Mixed Success
- Negative immediate ROI is bad for direct sales
- BUT: Awareness goals were met (100K impressions, 5K followers, 200% mention increase)
- High CPA ($100) only makes sense if CLV is high

**Recommendation:**
"If this was TRULY an awareness campaign, it succeeded (100K impressions, 5K followers). The negative sales ROI is expected for awareness.

However, $100 CPA is very high. Recommendation:
- ✅ **Keep running** if building awareness for long-term
- ⚠️ **Track those 5K new followers** - do they convert later?
- ⚠️ **Set clear non-sales KPIs** next time (impressions, followers, engagement)
- ❌ Don't judge awareness campaigns purely on sales ROI"

**Key Learning:** Different campaign types have different success criteria.

---

**SCENARIO 2: Product Launch**

**Calculations:**
- ROI = ($25,000 - $5,000) / $5,000 × 100 = **400%**
- CPA = $5,000 / 250 = **$20 per customer**

**Evaluation:**
✅ Strong Success
- 400% ROI = For every $1 spent, got $5 back
- $20 CPA for $100 product = $80 profit per sale
- 250 conversions is substantial volume

**Recommendation:**
"✅ **Scale up immediately!** This is working extremely well.

Actions:
- Increase budget by 50-100%
- Identify which channel performed best and double down
- Test similar creative/messaging
- Monitor to ensure performance holds at higher spend
- Expected outcome: 500-600 total sales if scaled properly"

**Key Learning:** When ROI is strong, scale quickly while it's working.

---

**SCENARIO 3: Retargeting**

**Calculations:**
- ROI = ($8,000 - $1,000) / $1,000 × 100 = **700%**
- CPA = $1,000 / 80 = **$12.50 per customer**

**Evaluation:**
✅✅ Excellent Success
- 700% ROI = For every $1 spent, got $8 back
- $12.50 CPA for $100 product = $87.50 profit per sale
- This is the best performing campaign

**Recommendation:**
"✅✅ **SCALE AGGRESSIVELY!** Best ROI of all campaigns.

Actions:
- Increase retargeting budget by 100-200%
- Expand retargeting audience (30-day vs 7-day window)
- Test longer retargeting window
- Apply learnings to other campaigns
- This is your most efficient acquisition channel

Why it works: Retargeting reaches people already interested (warm audience)"

**Key Learning:** Retargeting often has best ROI because audience is warm.

---

**SCENARIO 4: Failed Flash Sale**

**Calculations:**
- ROI = ($1,200 - $3,000) / $3,000 × 100 = **-60%**
- CPA = $3,000 / 20 = **$150 per customer**

**Evaluation:**
❌ Clear Failure
- Lost 60% of investment
- $150 CPA for $60 product = losing $90 per sale
- Only 20 conversions despite high traffic

**Recommendation:**
"❌ **Stop this campaign immediately.** Hemorrhaging money.

Why it failed:
- CPA ($150) higher than product price ($60)
- Many views but few conversions = messaging/offer problem
- Could be: Wrong audience, weak offer, poor landing page, pricing issue

Next steps:
- Pause immediately to stop losses
- Investigate: Was it targeting? Creative? Offer? Landing page?
- Test on smaller budget ($500) with changes before trying again
- Consider: Maybe this product doesn't work at this price point

Do NOT just try again without understanding why it failed."

**Key Learning:** When losing money, stop and diagnose before continuing.

---

### Review Script (After Lunch - 5 min)

**Instructor:**

"Welcome back! Let's quickly review the ROI scenarios from this morning.

**Scenario 1 (Awareness):** -50% ROI, $100 CPA
- ✅ Judge by awareness metrics (impressions, followers), not sales ROI
- Set appropriate KPIs for awareness campaigns

**Scenario 2 (Launch):** 400% ROI, $20 CPA
- ✅✅ Scale up! This is working.

**Scenario 3 (Retargeting):** 700% ROI, $12.50 CPA
- ✅✅✅ Best performer - increase budget significantly

**Scenario 4 (Failed):** -60% ROI, $150 CPA
- ❌ Stop immediately, diagnose problem

**Key Lessons:**
1. Context matters - awareness ≠ conversion campaigns
2. When something works (400%+ ROI), scale it
3. Retargeting often outperforms cold traffic
4. When losing money, pause and investigate

Questions?"

### Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Math errors in ROI calculation  
**Solution:** Check if they're subtracting investment first: (Rev - Inv) / Inv

**Issue:** Not understanding if ROI is good or bad  
**Solution:** "Would you want to invest $1 to get back $4? That's 400% ROI!"

**Issue:** Can't see why Scenario 1 could be successful  
**Solution:** "What if your goal wasn't sales, but getting 100K people to hear about your new product?"

**Issue:** Saying to scale Scenario 4  
**Solution:** "You're losing $90 on every sale. How is that sustainable?"

### Extension Activity

"Calculate Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) for Scenario 2:
- If average customer makes 3 purchases per year
- And stays a customer for 2 years
- Average order value is $100

CLV = $100 × 3 × 2 = $600

Now the $20 CPA looks even better! Lifetime ROI = ($600 - $20) / $20 × 100 = 2,900%"

## 📄 ACTIVITY 4: Report Makeover Challenge

**Time:** 25 minutes (20 min work + 5 min presentations)  
**Format:** Pairs  
**Timing:** 1:55-2:15 PM (after reporting lecture)

### Objectives

By the end of this activity, students will:
- Identify common report mistakes
- Apply reporting best practices
- Create executive-ready one-page reports
- Distill data into clear narratives

### Materials Needed

**Per Pair:**
- "Bad report" example (printed)
- Blank one-page executive template
- Access to Day 2 data for charts (if needed)
- Markers/highlighters

**Bad Report Contains:**
- 15 different metrics with no context
- Ugly, confusing 3D pie chart
- No executive summary
- Technical jargon
- No recommendations
- Poor formatting
- Buried key insights

### Setup Instructions

**Before Activity:**
1. Print bad report example (1 per pair)
2. Print blank executive template (1 per pair)
3. Ensure chart creation tools available (Excel, pens, paper)
4. Prepare timer

**Room Setup:**
- Students work in pairs (assigned or self-selected)
- Access to Day 2 materials for visualization reference

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Instructor Script:**

"You'll receive a poorly designed report with multiple problems. This is the kind of report that gets ignored by executives.

Working in pairs, you have 20 minutes to:

**1. Identify what's wrong (5 min)**
- List all the problems you see
- Think: would a busy CEO read this?

**2. Redesign as one-page executive report (15 min)**
- Create executive summary (1 paragraph)
- Choose 3-5 key metrics only
- Design or sketch one clear chart
- Write specific recommendations
- Professional formatting

Use the template provided or create your own layout.

**Remember from the lecture:**
- Lead with main point
- Less is more (max 5 metrics)
- Clear narrative
- Actionable recommendations
- Professional design

Ready? Begin!"

**During Activity:**
- Set timer for 20 minutes
- Circulate between pairs
- Ask probing questions:
  - "What's your main message?"
  - "Is this the most important metric to show?"
  - "Can you cut this down further?"
  - "What would a CEO care about here?"
- Give 5-minute and 2-minute warnings

**Coaching Questions:**
- "You have 10 metrics. Which 3 are most critical?"
- "What's the one insight an executive must know?"
- "How does this recommendation connect to business outcomes?"

**Problems with This Report:**
1. ❌ No executive summary
2. ❌ Too many metrics (15!) - overwhelming
3. ❌ No context (is 6.8% good?)
4. ❌ Ugly 3D pie chart
5. ❌ No clear narrative
6. ❌ Vague recommendations ("maybe post more")
7. ❌ Buries key insight (video performance)
8. ❌ No comparison to targets or benchmarks
9. ❌ No specific actions
10. ❌ Poor formatting (wall of numbers)

### Example Good Report (For Instructor Reference)

```
SOCIAL MEDIA PERFORMANCE REPORT
February 2026 | Executive Summary

HEADLINE:
Engagement increased 35% in February, driven by video content. 
Recommend shifting 30% more budget to video production for 
continued growth.

KEY METRICS:
• Engagement Rate: 6.8% (↑35% vs Jan, ↑79% vs industry avg 3.8%)
  → Exceeds target of 6.0%
• Reach: 47,892 (↑7% vs Jan)
  → On track to 50K monthly target
• Follower Growth: +643 net (↑2.3%)
  → Slightly below target of 3%

[Clean 2D bar chart showing engagement by content type:
Reels: 9.2%
Carousels: 7.1%
Static: 4.2%
Stories: 6.0%]

INSIGHTS:
1. Video content (Reels) drives 2× higher engagement than static posts
2. Instagram performance outpaces Facebook (8.1% vs 3.2% engagement)
3. Posting frequency optimized at 7×/week (previous test confirmed)

RECOMMENDATIONS:
1. Increase video production budget by $2,000
   → Expected impact: +20% engagement growth
2. Shift 40% of content to Reels (currently 25%)
   → Capitalize on highest-performing format
3. Reduce Facebook posting from 5×/week to 3×/week
   → Reallocate time to Instagram Reels production

Next Month Target: 7.5% engagement rate, 50K reach
```

**Why This Works:**
✅ Clear executive summary upfront
✅ Only 3 key metrics with context
✅ Clean, professional chart
✅ Specific insights backed by data
✅ Actionable recommendations with expected impact
✅ Scannable in 60 seconds

**After 20 Minutes:**

**Debrief (2 min):**

**What made the difference?**
- Focus (3-5 metrics, not 15)
- Story (clear narrative, not data dump)
- Action (specific recommendations, not vague)
- Design (clean, professional, not cluttered)

This is the skill that makes you valuable—translating data into decisions executives can act on immediately.

The bad report took hours to create and would get ignored. The good report takes the same time but gets read and acted on."

### Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Pairs still including too many metrics  
**Solution:** "Force yourself to choose only 3. Which 3 matter MOST?"

**Issue:** No clear narrative  
**Solution:** "Complete this sentence: 'The most important thing is...'"

**Issue:** Struggling with chart

## 🎤 ACTIVITY 5: 2-Minute Presentation Practice

**Time:** 20 minutes (15 min practice + 5 min optional demos)  
**Format:** Pairs, then volunteer presentations  
**Timing:** 2:30-2:45 PM (after presentation lecture)

### Objectives

By the end of this activity, students will:
- Structure a 2-minute presentation effectively
- Deliver insights concisely
- Handle questions confidently
- Use proper presentation techniques

### Materials Needed

**Per Student:**
- Scenario card with key data points
- Timer (visible to pairs)
- Presentation structure template

**Three Scenarios:**
- Scenario A: Q1 results (positive)
- Scenario B: Mixed campaign performance
- Scenario C: Budget increase request

### Setup Instructions

**Before Activity:**
1. Print scenario cards (divide class into thirds)
2. Prepare timer
3. Write presentation structure on board
4. Clear space for standing presentations (optional)

**Structure on Board:**
```
2-MINUTE PITCH STRUCTURE

0:00-0:20  HOOK (Main finding)
0:20-1:00  EVIDENCE (Key data)
1:00-1:30  INSIGHT (What it means)
1:30-2:00  ACTION (What to do)
```

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Instructor Script:**

"Find a partner. You'll each give a 2-minute presentation on one of three scenarios. Your partner plays the 'executive' role—they'll listen and ask 1-2 questions after.

**How it works:**

**1. Prepare (3 min)**
- Read your scenario
- Organize using the structure: Hook → Evidence → Insight → Action
- Mental outline, no script

**2. Person A Presents (2 min)**
- I'll time exactly 2 minutes
- Use the structure we learned
- Pretend you're presenting to your boss

**3. Person B Asks Questions (1 min)**
- Ask 1-2 challenging questions
- Person A answers

**4. Switch (3 min total)**
- Person B presents their scenario
- Person A asks questions

**5. Feedback (2 min)**
- Give each other one thing that worked well
- One thing to improve

I'll time strictly. When I say '2 minutes,' you must wrap up immediately—that's the executive world!

Ready? Find a partner and grab a scenario card!"

**During Activity:**

**Minutes 0-3:** Students prepare
- Circulate, answer questions about scenarios
- Ensure they understand the structure
- Encourage: "Don't memorize—outline"

**Minutes 3-5:** Round 1 Person A presents
- Call "Begin!" and start timer
- Walk around listening to various pairs
- Note strong performers for potential demo

**At 2:00 Mark:** Call "Time! Wrap it up!"
- This teaches them to respect time limits

**Minutes 5-6:** Person B asks questions, Person A responds

**Minutes 6-9:** Round 2 Person B presents (repeat process)

**Minutes 9-10:** Person A asks questions, Person B responds

**Minutes 10-12:** Pairs give each other feedback

**Coaching While Observing:**
- Listen for structure (are they following Hook-Evidence-Insight-Action?)
- Note body language (eye contact, posture)
- Identify nervous habits (filler words, fidgeting)
- Find good examples to showcase

### Scenario Cards

```

### Optional Volunteer Demo (5 min)

**If time permits and you have willing volunteers:**

"Great practice! Would anyone like to present to the whole class? Imagine we're all the executives you're presenting to."

**If volunteer emerges:**
1. Have them stand at front
2. Class plays executive role
3. Student presents 2-minute pitch
4. Class asks 2-3 questions
5. Instructor provides positive, constructive feedback

**Feedback Framework:**
"What worked well:
- [Specific positive: 'You led with the main point immediately']
- [Specific positive: 'Your recommendations were specific and actionable']

One thing to refine:
- [Constructive: 'You could slow down a bit—you have time']
- [Tip: 'Try pausing after key points for emphasis']

Overall: [Encouraging summary]"

**If no volunteers (common):**
"That's okay—presenting is scary! You did great practicing with partners. The structure works—keep using it."

### Debrief (3 min)

**Instructor:**

"How did that feel? Harder than it looks, right?

**Show of hands:**
- Who went over 2 minutes? (Most will)
- Who felt they nailed the structure?
- Who got a tough question they couldn't answer?

**Key Takeaways:**

**1. Two minutes goes FAST**
You think you have time to build up—you don't. Lead with the punchline.

**2. Structure helps enormously**
Hook-Evidence-Insight-Action keeps you organized even when nervous.

**3. Practice makes it smoother**
Your first time was probably choppy. Do it 10 times and you'll be smooth.

**4. Questions are okay**
'I don't know, but I'll find out' is a perfectly good answer.

**5. Confidence comes with reps**
The framework is solid. Now you just need practice.

**Pro tip:** Practice this with real data at work. Ask your colleague, 'Give me 2 minutes to tell you about last month's results.' It builds the skill."

### Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Student way over 2 minutes  
**Solution:** Interrupt at 2:00 mark—teaches hard stop reality

**Issue:** Student freezes/blanks  
**Solution:** Partner prompts: "What was your main finding?"

**Issue:** Too much detail in evidence section  
**Solution:** "You only have 40 seconds for evidence—pick the top 3 numbers"

**Issue:** No clear recommendation  
**Solution:** "End with: 'I recommend we...' and be specific"

**Issue:** Speaking too fast (nerves)  
**Solution:** "It's okay to pause and breathe. Pauses feel longer to you than to listeners."

**Issue:** Reading from notes  
**Solution:** "Glance at notes okay, but talk TO them, not READ to them"

### Extension Activity

"Record yourself giving this presentation on your phone. Watch it back. It's painful but educational. You'll see: filler words, pacing issues, body language. Then do it again and compare."

1. Form groups of 3-4 students (count off or self-select)

3. Distribute discussion questions (1 per group)

- Groups gather in different areas of room

- "What made Dove's campaign measurable?"

### Common Questions & How to Respond

**Q: "I don't know where to start."**
**A:** "Start with data exploration. Open it up, look at what's there. Then ask: what jumps out as interesting? What questions does this data answer?"

**Q: "Which metrics should I focus on?"**
**A:** "Think: if you were presenting to a CEO, what would matter most? Business outcomes—revenue, ROI, growth. Start there, then support with engagement metrics."

**Q: "Is this insight good enough?"**
**A:** [Review their insight] "Does it answer 'so what?' If I'm an executive, do I care? Can I act on it? If yes, it's good."

**Q: "How many charts should I include?"**
**A:** "Quality over quantity. I'd rather see 2 excellent charts that tell a clear story than 5 mediocre ones. Probably 3-4 for the full report."

**Q: "Is my ROI calculation correct?"**
**A:** "Walk me through your formula... [check their work] ...yes, that's right!" OR "Let's check: what's your revenue minus investment? Now divide by investment and multiply by 100."

**Q: "This is overwhelming."**
**A:** "I know—real data is messy! Break it down: tackle one section at a time. Start with overall performance, then dig into what drove it. One step at a time."

**Q: "Can we work together?"**
**A:** "Discussion is great—share ideas, talk through insights. But everyone submits their own analysis. Your report should be your own work."

**Q: "Do I need to use all the data?"**
**A:** "No! Use what supports your narrative. Everything else can go in an appendix. Focus on the most important insights."

**Q: "How do I know if my recommendation is good?"**
**A:** "Ask yourself: Is it specific? Is it actionable? Is it backed by data? Does it have expected impact? If yes to all, it's good."

**Q: "I'm stuck on [technical issue]."**
**A:** [Help troubleshoot Excel, calculations, etc.]

## 📝 GENERAL ACTIVITY TIPS

### Timing Management
- Start activities on time
- Give warnings (5 min, 2 min, time's up)
- Be firm about time limits (respects those who finished)
- Have extension activities for early finishers

### Engagement Strategies
- Circulate during every activity
- Make eye contact
- Ask questions, don't lecture
- Celebrate good work publicly
- Provide individual encouragement

### Dealing with Struggles
- Normalize difficulty ("This is challenging for everyone")
- Provide hints, not answers
- Break complex tasks into smaller steps
- Pair struggling students with confident ones

### Creating Safe Environment
- Make mistakes okay
- Volunteer presentations should be optional
- Praise effort and thinking, not just correct answers
- Private feedback for sensitive issues

### Energy Management
- High energy for launches
- Quiet presence during work time
- Re-energize for discussions
- Match your energy to activity type

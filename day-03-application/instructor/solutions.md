## 1️⃣ MORNING EXERCISE SOLUTIONS

### Exercise 1: Competitive Matrix (15 minutes)

**Scenario:** Three coffee shop brands competing in same market
- Brand A (You): 25K followers, 5.2% engagement, posts 5×/week
- Brand B: 45K followers, 3.8% engagement, posts 7×/week  
- Brand C: 18K followers, 6.1% engagement, posts 4×/week

**Questions & Solutions:**

**Q1: Create a competitive matrix comparing all three brands**

**Solution:**

| Metric | Brand A (You) | Brand B | Brand C | Your Position |
|--------|---------------|---------|---------|---------------|
| Followers | 25,000 | 45,000 | 18,000 | 2nd of 3 |
| Engagement Rate | 5.2% | 3.8% | 6.1% | 2nd of 3 |
| Posting Frequency | 5/week | 7/week | 4/week | 2nd of 3 |
| Eng. per 1K | 52 | 38 | 61 | 2nd of 3 |
| Est. Weekly Eng. | 1,300 | 1,710 | 1,098 | 2nd of 3 |

**Analysis:**
- Brand A is middle-of-the-pack across most metrics
- Brand B leads in reach but has lowest engagement rate
- Brand C has highest engagement rate but smallest audience

---

**Q2: What is each competitor's strength?**

**Solution:**

**Brand B's Strength:** Audience Size & Reach
- 45K followers (80% more than you, 150% more than Brand C)
- Highest posting frequency (7×/week) = consistent presence
- Generates most total weekly engagement (~1,710)
- Likely established market leader
- Strategy: Volume and visibility

**Brand C's Strength:** Engagement Quality
- Highest engagement rate (6.1%)
- Best engagement per 1,000 followers (61 vs your 52)
- Most efficient content (less posts, better performance)
- Likely has loyal, niche community
- Strategy: Quality over quantity

**Your Strength:** Balanced Approach
- Middle ground on all metrics
- Room to grow in multiple directions
- Not over-posting or under-posting
- Consistent but not exceptional

---

**Q3: What opportunities can you identify?**

**Solution:**

**Opportunity 1: Learn from Brand C's Engagement Strategy**
- Analyze what content drives their 6.1% rate
- Study their audience interaction patterns
- Test similar approaches with your audience
- Goal: Improve your 5.2% to 6%+

**Opportunity 2: Close the Follower Gap with Brand B**
- You have better engagement (5.2% vs 3.8%)
- Can grow audience while maintaining quality
- Opportunity: Reach Brand B's audience with better content
- Goal: Hit 30K followers within 6 months

**Opportunity 3: Find Underserved Content Niches**
- What topics are competitors not covering?
- Are there audience questions going unanswered?
- Can you own a specific content type? (e.g., coffee education, sustainability)

**Opportunity 4: Leverage Higher Engagement for Partnerships**
- Your 5.2% beats Brand B's 3.8%
- More appealing to potential partners/sponsors
- Can command better collaboration terms

**Opportunity 5: Optimize Posting Frequency**
- You post 5×/week (between Brand C's 4 and Brand B's 7)
- Test: Could 6×/week increase total engagement without hurting rate?
- Find your sweet spot

---

**Q4: What's one specific strategy you'd implement based on this analysis?**

**Possible Strong Answers (accept variations):**

**Strategy Option 1: Engagement-Focused Growth**
"Analyze Brand C's top 10 performing posts to identify what drives their 6.1% engagement rate. Test similar content themes, formats, and CTAs for 4 weeks while maintaining current posting frequency. Goal: Increase engagement rate from 5.2% to 5.8% while growing followers by 2,000."

**Why this works:**
- Specific and measurable
- Based on data (Brand C's success)
- Has timeline and clear goal
- Balances growth with engagement quality

**Strategy Option 2: Content Gap Targeting**
"Conduct content audit of all three brands to find topics they don't cover. Create a content series around that gap (e.g., 'Coffee Science' or 'Bean Origins') posting 2×/week for 8 weeks. Promote to competitors' audiences. Goal: Establish thought leadership and gain 3,000 new followers."

**Why this works:**
- Differentiates from competitors
- Creates unique value
- Strategic audience targeting
- Clear success metrics

**Strategy Option 3: Engagement Rate Optimization**
"Maintain current audience size focus on drastically improving engagement from 5.2% to 6.5% (matching Brand C). Analyze your own bottom 25% of posts, eliminate those content types. Double down on your top performers. Test optimal posting times. Result: Higher engagement rate makes you more attractive than both competitors."

**Why this works:**
- Quality over quantity focus
- Data-driven approach
- Leverages existing assets
- Creates competitive advantage

### Exercise 2: Campaign Attribution (20 minutes)

**Scenario:** Valentine's Day campaign results
- Campaign ran Feb 1-14
- Total campaign-related sales: $12,450
- Campaign spend: $2,500

**Traffic & Conversion Data:**
- Instagram ad clicks: 2,400 → 180 purchases = 7.5% conversion
- Influencer post: 1,800 clicks → 120 purchases = 6.7% conversion  
- Email (with social links): 3,200 clicks → 210 purchases = 6.6% conversion
- Organic social: 4,100 clicks → 150 purchases = 3.7% conversion

**Questions & Solutions:**

**Q1: Using last-click attribution, which channel gets credit for most sales?**

**Solution:**

**Last-Click Attribution = Credit goes to final touchpoint before purchase**

Distribution (based on purchases):
- Email: 210 purchases = 31.8% of sales credit
- Instagram ads: 180 purchases = 27.3% of sales credit
- Organic social: 150 purchases = 22.7% of sales credit
- Influencer: 120 purchases = 18.2% of sales credit

**Answer: Email gets most credit (210 purchases, 31.8%)**

Revenue Attribution:
- Email: $12,450 × 31.8% = $3,959
- Instagram ads: $12,450 × 27.3% = $3,399
- Organic social: $12,450 × 22.7% = $2,826
- Influencer: $12,450 × 18.2% = $2,266

**Insight:** Email had highest conversion count but this may not tell full story (users might have discovered brand elsewhere first).

---

**Q2: Calculate the conversion rate for each source**

**Solution:**

Formula: Conversion Rate = (Purchases / Clicks) × 100

**Instagram Ads:**
(180 / 2,400) × 100 = **7.5%** ← HIGHEST

**Influencer Post:**
(120 / 1,800) × 100 = **6.7%**

**Email:**
(210 / 3,200) × 100 = **6.6%**

**Organic Social:**
(150 / 4,100) × 100 = **3.7%** ← LOWEST

**Analysis:**
- Instagram ads have highest conversion rate (7.5%)
- Organic social has lowest rate (3.7%)
- Paid channels (ads, influencer) outperform organic
- Email performs well with high volume + decent rate

---

**Q3: Which channel provided best ROI?**

**Solution:**

**Need to allocate campaign spend first:**

Total spend: $2,500
- Instagram ads: $1,500 (paid directly)
- Influencer: $800 (paid collaboration)
- Email: $150 (email platform cost)
- Organic: $50 (content creation time)
**Total: $2,500**

**ROI Calculation: ((Revenue - Cost) / Cost) × 100**

**Instagram Ads:**
Revenue: $3,399
Cost: $1,500
ROI: (($3,399 - $1,500) / $1,500) × 100 = **127%** ✓

**Influencer:**
Revenue: $2,266
Cost: $800
ROI: (($2,266 - $800) / $800) × 100 = **183%** ← BEST ROI ✓✓✓

**Email:**
Revenue: $3,959
Cost: $150
ROI: (($3,959 - $150) / $150) × 100 = **2,539%** ← INCREDIBLE! ✓✓✓✓✓

**Organic Social:**
Revenue: $2,826
Cost: $50
ROI: (($2,826 - $50) / $50) × 100 = **5,552%** ← BEST! ✓✓✓✓✓✓

**Answer: Organic social provided best ROI (5,552%), followed by email (2,539%)**

**Key Insight:** 
- Lowest cost channels can have highest ROI
- But they may not scale (can't just "buy more" organic reach)
- Instagram ads have lower ROI (127%) but are scalable
- Balanced strategy uses both high-ROI organic and scalable paid

---

**Q4: How would you allocate next campaign's budget?**

**Strong Answer Example:**

"Based on the ROI analysis, I'd allocate the budget as follows for the next campaign:

**Total Budget: $3,500 (40% increase)**

**Email: $250** (+$100 increase)
- Current ROI: 2,539% (incredible)
- Increase to segment audience better
- Test personalization
- Add abandoned cart recovery

**Influencer: $1,200** (+$400 increase)
- Current ROI: 183% (strong)
- Worked well, scale up
- Test 2 micro-influencers instead of 1 mid-tier
- Better targeting + lower cost

**Instagram Ads: $1,800** (+$300 increase)
- Current ROI: 127% (good, scalable)
- Highest conversion rate (7.5%)
- Increase budget but optimize targeting
- A/B test creative

**Organic Social Content: $250** (+$200 increase)
- Best ROI (5,552%) but limited scale
- Invest in better content creation
- Hire photographer or designer
- Create more shareable content

**Reasoning:**
- Double down on what works (influencer, email)
- Scale paid channels moderately (Instagram)
- Invest in organic content quality (multiplier effect)
- Maintain balanced approach for attribution"

**Why this answer is strong:**
- ✓ Data-driven decisions
- ✓ Considers both ROI and scalability
- ✓ Specific budget allocations
- ✓ Strategic reasoning
- ✓ Tests and optimizations planned

## 🎯 ACTIVITY 2: CAMPAIGN TRACKING EXERCISE SOLUTIONS

### Scenario: Summer product launch campaign

**Campaign:** SunGlow sunscreen launch (June 1-30)
**Budget:** $5,000
**Goal:** 500 sales at $25/unit = $12,500 revenue target

**Results by Channel:**

| Channel | Spend | Clicks | Conversions | Revenue |
|---------|-------|--------|-------------|---------|
| Facebook Ads | $2,000 | 5,000 | 200 | $5,000 |
| Instagram Ads | $1,500 | 4,200 | 180 | $4,500 |
| Influencer (3 posts) | $1,200 | 2,800 | 85 | $2,125 |
| Email Campaign | $200 | 1,500 | 90 | $2,250 |
| Organic Social | $100 | 3,500 | 65 | $1,625 |

**Totals:** $5,000 spend | 17,000 clicks | 620 conversions | $15,500 revenue

---

### SOLUTION: Complete Campaign Analysis

#### Part 1: Calculate Key Metrics for Each Channel

**Formulas:**
- CTR = Clicks / (Spend / $2 avg CPM impression cost) - simplified for this exercise
- Conversion Rate = (Conversions / Clicks) × 100
- CPC = Spend / Clicks
- Cost Per Conversion = Spend / Conversions
- ROI = ((Revenue - Spend) / Spend) × 100

---

**Facebook Ads:**
- Spend: $2,000
- Clicks: 5,000
- Conversions: 200
- Revenue: $5,000

**Metrics:**
- **CPC:** $2,000 / 5,000 = **$0.40**
- **Conversion Rate:** (200 / 5,000) × 100 = **4.0%**
- **Cost per Conversion:** $2,000 / 200 = **$10.00**
- **Revenue per Conversion:** $5,000 / 200 = $25.00 (product price)
- **ROI:** (($5,000 - $2,000) / $2,000) × 100 = **150%** ✓✓

**Analysis:** Strong performance. Breaking even at 80 conversions (80 × $25 = $2,000), achieved 200. Efficient and scalable.

---

**Instagram Ads:**
- Spend: $1,500
- Clicks: 4,200
- Conversions: 180
- Revenue: $4,500

**Metrics:**
- **CPC:** $1,500 / 4,200 = **$0.36** ← Best CPC
- **Conversion Rate:** (180 / 4,200) × 100 = **4.3%** ← Best conversion rate
- **Cost per Conversion:** $1,500 / 180 = **$8.33** ← Best cost per conversion
- **ROI:** (($4,500 - $1,500) / $1,500) × 100 = **200%** ← Best ROI ✓✓✓

**Analysis:** Best performing channel across almost all metrics. Most efficient ad spend. Priority for scaling.

---

---

**SOLUTION:**

**Step 1: Calculate engagement improvement**
- Before: 15 posts × 350 = 5,250 total monthly engagement
- After: 15 posts × 520 = 7,800 total monthly engagement
- Increase: 7,800 - 5,250 = 2,550 more engagements (+49%)

**Step 2: Determine value of increased engagement**

This is subjective but defensible approaches:

**Approach A: Value per engagement**
- Assume average engagement value: $0.50 (clicks to website, brand awareness)
- Value gained: 2,550 × $0.50 = $1,275

**ROI Calculation:**
ROI = (($1,275 - $800) / $800) × 100 = **59% ROI**

---

**Approach B: Conversion-based**
- Assume 2% of engagements lead to website visit
- Assume 5% of visits convert at $40 average order
- Before: 5,250 × 0.02 × 0.05 × $40 = $210 revenue
- After: 7,800 × 0.02 × 0.05 × $40 = $312 revenue
- Gain: $312 - $210 = $102

**ROI Calculation:**
ROI = (($102 - $800) / $800) × 100 = **-87% ROI** (loss)

*Note: This assumes ONLY direct sales. Doesn't account for brand awareness, follower growth, etc.*

**Approach C: Follower growth value**
- Better visuals led to 800 new followers (during campaign)
- Industry avg: New follower worth $2 in lifetime value
- Value: 800 × $2 = $1,600

**ROI Calculation:**
ROI = (($1,600 - $800) / $800) × 100 = **100% ROI**

---

**Best Answer:**
"The photographer investment generated **59-100% ROI** depending on attribution model. Tangible benefits include +49% engagement increase (2,550 more engagements) and +800 new followers. **Question 1:**
You're analyzing two competitors. Competitor A has 100K followers and 2.5% engagement rate. Competitor B has 45K followers and 5.8% engagement rate. Who has better engagement per 1,000 followers?

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:**
- Competitor A: 2.5% = 25 per 1,000 (2.5% × 10 = 25)
- Competitor B: 5.8% = 58 per 1,000 (5.8% × 10 = 58)

**Question 2:**
A campaign cost $5,000 and generated $18,000 in revenue. What's the ROI?

A) 140%
B) 260%
C) 360%
D) 460%

**Answer: B**

**Calculation:**
ROI = ((Revenue - Cost) / Cost) × 100
ROI = (($18,000 - $5,000) / $5,000) × 100
ROI = ($13,000 / $5,000) × 100
ROI = 2.6 × 100 = 260%

**Question 3:**
In last-click attribution, which touchpoint gets credit for the sale?

A) The first touchpoint (where customer discovered brand)
B) The touchpoint with most engagement
C) The final touchpoint before purchase
D) Credit is split equally across all touchpoints

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:**
Last-click attribution gives 100% credit to the final interaction before conversion. This is simple but can undervalue earlier touchpoints in the customer journey (like awareness campaigns or initial influencer discovery).

**Question 4:**
You're presenting to the CEO. What should you lead with?

A) Methodology explanation
B) Bottom-line results
C) Detailed metrics
D) Competitive analysis

**Explanation:**

**Question 5:**
Which metric best shows you're improving audience quality, not just quantity?

A) Follower count
B) Impressions
C) Engagement rate
D) Reach

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:**
Engagement rate adjusts for audience size, showing what percentage of your audience actually interacts. You can grow followers (quantity) while engagement rate drops (quality). A growing engagement rate means your audience is becoming more engaged, not just larger.

**Question 6:**
Your Instagram Shopping feature generated 80 sales at $30 average with zero ad spend. What's the ROI?

A) 0% (no profit)
B) 100%
C) 2,400%
D) Infinite

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:**
ROI = ((Revenue - Cost) / Cost) × 100
When cost = $0, you divide by zero, which is undefined/infinite. The practical answer: any revenue with zero cost = infinite ROI. (For grading: Accept both D "Infinite" and C "2,400%" if student calculates using minimal costs like $100 for setup.)

Executives care about business impact. Revenue and ROI directly tie social media to company performance. Other metrics matter but should support the main point: did this make money?

**Question 8:**
You spend $2,000 on a campaign that generates 400 conversions at $20 average order value. What's your cost per conversion?

B) $5

**Question 9:**
Your competitor posts 10 times per week and gets 500 avg engagement. You post 5 times per week and get 400 avg engagement. Who has better engagement per post?

A) Your competitor (500 vs 400)
B) You (800 vs 500 per post)
C) Equal efficiency
D) Need more data to determine

**Answer: B**

**Calculation:**
- Your competitor: 500 avg engagement per post (given directly)
- You: Need to calculate total then divide
  - Wait - the question says 400 avg engagement (per post assumed)
  - But if competitor gets 500 per post and you get 400 per post, competitor wins

**Actually, let me re-read:**
"Competitor posts 10×/week, 500 avg engagement"
"You post 5×/week, 400 avg engagement"

**Interpretation A: These are averages per post**
- Competitor: 500 per post
- You: 400 per post
- Competitor wins

**Interpretation B: These are weekly totals**
- Competitor: 500 total / 10 posts = 50 per post

- Competitor: 500 / 10 = 50 per post

**Question 10:**
What's the main limitation of last-click attribution?

Last-click attribution gives 100% credit to the final touchpoint before purchase. This means if someone:
1. Discovers brand via influencer post (awareness)
2. Sees Facebook ad (consideration)
3. Receives email reminder (decision)
4. Clicks email and purchases

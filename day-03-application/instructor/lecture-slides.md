### SLIDE 11: SOV Formula
**Visual:** Formula with example

```
CALCULATING SHARE OF VOICE

SOV = (Your Mentions / Total Industry Mentions) × 100

EXAMPLE:
Your brand: 5,000 mentions
Competitor A: 8,000
Competitor B: 6,000
Competitor C: 4,000
Competitor D: 2,000
─────────────────
Total: 25,000 mentions

Your SOV = 5,000 / 25,000 × 100 = 20%
```

**Presenter Notes:**
"Simple formula, powerful insight. In this example, you have 20% of the conversation. But is that good? Depends on your market share and goals."

### SLIDE 21: The 5 UTM Parameters
**Visual:** Five labeled tags

```
UTM PARAMETER BREAKDOWN

utm_source
→ WHERE? (facebook, instagram, twitter, email)

utm_medium
→ WHAT TYPE? (social, cpc, email, referral)

utm_campaign
→ WHICH CAMPAIGN? (summer_sale_2026)

utm_content
→ SPECIFIC CONTENT (carousel_ad, story_1, feed_post)

utm_term
→ KEYWORDS (optional, mainly for paid search)
```

**Presenter Notes:**
"These five parameters answer: where did they come from, what brought them, which campaign, what specific content, and if relevant, what search term. First three are essential. Content adds detail. Term is mainly for paid search."

### SLIDE 23: UTM Best Practices
**Visual:** Checklist with do's and don'ts

```
UTM NAMING CONVENTIONS

DO:
✓ Use lowercase only
✓ Use underscores (not spaces): summer_sale
✓ Be consistent: always "instagram" not "ig" or "insta"
✓ Be specific: story_ad_1, story_ad_2
✓ Document your conventions

DON'T:
✗ Mix uppercase/lowercase: Instagram vs instagram
✗ Use spaces: "summer sale"
✗ Change naming mid-campaign
✗ Make it too generic: post1, post2
✗ Forget to tag!

Consistency = Clean data = Useful insights
```

**Presenter Notes:**
"This might seem nitpicky, but inconsistent naming creates data chaos. If you use 'instagram', 'Instagram', and 'IG', analytics sees three different sources. Pick conventions and stick to them."

### SLIDE 32: Basic ROI Formula
**Visual:** Formula highlighted

```
ROI FORMULA

ROI = (Revenue - Investment) / Investment × 100

COMPONENTS:
Revenue = Income generated
Investment = Total costs (ads + tools + labor)

EXAMPLE:
Revenue: $15,000
Investment: $5,000

ROI = ($15,000 - $5,000) / $5,000 × 100
ROI = $10,000 / $5,000 × 100
ROI = 200%

For every $1 spent → $3 back ($1 original + $2 profit)
```

**Presenter Notes:**
"This is the fundamental formula executives know. 200% ROI means you tripled your money. 100% means you doubled it. 0% means you broke even. Negative means you lost money."

### SLIDE 35: Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)
**Visual:** Formula with icons

```
COST PER ACQUISITION

CPA = Total Campaign Cost / Number of Conversions

EXAMPLE:
Campaign cost: $3,000
Purchases: 150

CPA = $3,000 / 150 = $20 per customer

QUESTION: Is $20 good or bad?
→ Depends on what you're selling!
```

**Presenter Notes:**
"CPA is simpler than ROI—just: how much did each customer cost? But judging if it's good requires context."

### SLIDE 36: Evaluating CPA
**Visual:** Good vs Bad CPA examples

```
IS YOUR CPA GOOD?

SCENARIO A: Selling $15 t-shirts

SCENARIO B: Selling $100 shoes
CPA = $20 per customer

```

**Presenter Notes:**

### SLIDE 37: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)
**Visual:** Customer journey timeline

```
CUSTOMER LIFETIME VALUE

Not just the first purchase—all future value.

EXAMPLE: Subscription Box
• First order: $40
• Orders per year: 6
• Customer stays: 2 years

CLV = $40 × 6 × 2 = $480

Now re-evaluate CPA:
• CPA: $20 to acquire
• CLV: $480 lifetime value
• Lifetime ROI: ($480-$20)/$20 × 100 = 2,300%

That $20 CPA looks amazing now!
```

**Presenter Notes:**
"Short-term ROI can look bad, but lifetime value tells a different story. That expensive acquisition is worth it if customers stick around. This is especially true for subscription businesses or high-repeat-purchase products."

---

### SLIDE 38: Simple CLV Formula
**Visual:** Formula breakdown

```
CALCULATING CLV

CLV = Avg Purchase × Purchase Frequency × Lifespan

STEPS:
1. Average purchase value
   ($40 per order)

2. Purchases per year
   (6 times)

3. Years as customer
   (2 years)

4. Multiply
   $40 × 6 × 2 = $480

ADVANCED:
Factor in profit margin, referral value, etc.
But simple version is powerful enough!
```

**Presenter Notes:**
"You don't need a PhD in math. This simple version is good enough for most businesses. Estimate these three numbers and multiply."

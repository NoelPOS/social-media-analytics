**10:15 - 10:25 AM: Lecture (10 min)**

**Slide 1-2: Why Competitive Analysis Matters**

"Before we optimize our own performance, we need to understand the landscape. Competitive analysis answers:
- Are we winning or losing?
- What are competitors doing that works?
- Where are the gaps we can exploit?
- How should we allocate resources?"

**Key Message:** Data without context is just numbers. Competitive analysis provides the context.

---

**Slide 3-5: Identifying Competitors**

"Who are your competitors? Three types:

**1. Direct Competitors**
- Same products/services, same audience
- Example: Coca-Cola vs. Pepsi

**2. Indirect Competitors**
- Different products, same audience need
- Example: Coca-Cola vs. Coffee shops (both satisfy thirst/refreshment)

**3. Aspirational Competitors**
- Brands you want to emulate
- May be bigger, different industry, but inspiring
- Example: Local gym looking at Nike's community building

**For most analyses, focus on 3-5 direct competitors. More than that gets overwhelming."**

**Teaching Tip:** Ask students to name competitors in a familiar industry (fast food, smartphones, streaming) to illustrate the three types.

---

**Slide 6-9: Key Competitive Metrics**

"What to track for each competitor:

**Audience Metrics:**
- Follower count (size of audience)
- Follower growth rate (momentum)
- Audience demographics (if available)

**Content Metrics:**
- Posting frequency (how often)
- Content types (video, images, stories)
- Average engagement rate
- Top-performing content themes

**Engagement Quality:**
- Engagement per 1,000 followers
- Comment sentiment
- Share of voice (we'll calculate this)

**Important:** Don't just copy what competitors do. Understand WHY it works, and whether it fits YOUR brand."

---

**Slide 10-12: Share of Voice (SOV)**

"Share of Voice = Your share of total conversation in your industry

**Formula:**
```
Your SOV = (Your Mentions / Total Industry Mentions) × 100
```

**Example:**
- Your brand mentions: 5,000
- Competitor A: 8,000
- Competitor B: 6,000
- Competitor C: 4,000
- Competitor D: 2,000
**Total:** 25,000

Your SOV = 5,000 / 25,000 × 100 = **20%**

**What This Tells You:**
- If SOV > market share: You're punching above your weight (good buzz)
- If SOV < market share: You're underperforming in conversation
- If SOV declining: Losing mindshare to competitors"

**On whiteboard:** Draw a simple pie chart showing the SOV distribution

---

**Slide 13-15: Competitive Benchmarking Matrix**

"Create a simple comparison table:

| Metric | You | Comp A | Comp B | Comp C |
|--------|-----|--------|--------|--------|
| Followers | 25K | 42K | 38K | 15K |
| Eng. Rate | 6.8% | 3.2% | 5.5% | 4.1% |
| Posts/Week | 7 | 12 | 5 | 8 |
| SOV | 20% | 32% | 28% | 12% |

**Insights from this example:**
- ✅ We have highest engagement rate (quality over quantity)
- ❌ We have lowest follower count (growth opportunity)
- ⚠️ Our SOV is lower than Comp A & B (need more visibility)

**Key Takeaway:** Don't compete on every metric. Find where you excel and double down."

**Slide 18-20: Campaign Goal Setting**

"Before tracking, define success:

**Example Campaign: Summer Sale**

**Business Goal:** Increase revenue by 20%

**Marketing Goal:** Drive 1,000 website purchases

**Social Media Goals:**
- Generate 10,000 website clicks
- Achieve 2% click-through rate
- Cost per acquisition < $15
- At least 100 conversions from social

See the hierarchy? Business → Marketing → Social Media goals should align."

**Teaching Tip:** Draw goal hierarchy pyramid on board: Business (top) → Marketing (middle) → Social (bottom)

---

**Slide 21-24: UTM Parameters**

"UTM parameters are tags you add to URLs to track exactly where traffic comes from.

**The 5 UTM Parameters:**

1. **utm_source** = Platform (facebook, instagram, twitter)
2. **utm_medium** = Type of traffic (social, email, cpc)
3. **utm_campaign** = Campaign name (summer_sale_2026)
4. **utm_content** = Specific content (carousel_ad, story_1)
5. **utm_term** = Keywords (optional, mainly for paid search)

**Example:**
```
www.example.com/sale?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=summer_sale_2026&utm_content=carousel_ad
```

When someone clicks this link, Google Analytics shows exactly:
- They came from Instagram
- Through a social post (not an ad)
- From your summer sale campaign
- Specifically from the carousel ad

**Best Practices:**
- Be consistent (all lowercase, use underscores)
- Be specific (story_1, story_2 instead of just 'story')
- Document your naming conventions
- Never change source/medium mid-campaign"

**On whiteboard:** Write out anatomy of a UTM URL, labeling each part

---

**Slide 25-28: Attribution Models**

"Attribution = giving credit for a conversion

**Problem:** Customer journey is complex

Example customer path:
1. Sees Facebook ad (first touch)
2. Visits website, doesn't buy
3. Sees Instagram post (middle touch)
4. Gets email reminder
5. Clicks email link, makes purchase (last touch)

**Which touchpoint gets credit?**

**Three Main Models:**

**1. First-Touch Attribution**
- Facebook ad gets 100% credit
- Good for: Understanding awareness
- Limitation: Ignores nurturing

**2. Last-Touch Attribution**
- Email gets 100% credit
- Good for: Understanding final conversion driver
- Limitation: Ignores journey
- (This is what most analytics defaults to)

**3. Multi-Touch Attribution**
- Credit split among all touchpoints
- Examples: 
  - Linear (25% each to all 4 touches)
  - Time decay (more recent gets more credit)
  - U-shaped (first and last get most)
- Good for: Full journey understanding
- Limitation: Complex to implement

**For Most People:** Start with last-touch (simple), add first-touch (awareness), graduate to multi-touch when sophisticated."

**Discussion:** "Which model do you think is most fair? Why?"

**11:30 - 11:45 AM: Lecture (15 min)**

**Slide 29-30: The ROI Question**

"The question you'll be asked most: 'What's the ROI?'

**What They're Really Asking:**
'Is social media worth the money we're spending?'

**The Challenge:**
Some benefits are hard to quantify:
- Brand awareness
- Community building
- Customer loyalty

But we CAN measure:
- Revenue generated
- Cost savings
- Customer acquisition cost"

---

**Slide 31-34: ROI Formula**

"**Basic ROI Formula:**
```
ROI = (Revenue - Investment) / Investment × 100
```

**Example:**
- Investment: $5,000 (ads + tools + labor)
- Revenue: $15,000 (tracked sales from social)

ROI = ($15,000 - $5,000) / $5,000 × 100 = **200%**

**Translation:** For every $1 spent, you got $3 back ($1 original + $2 profit)

**What's a Good ROI?**
- 100%+ is generally positive (doubling your money)
- 200%+ is excellent
- Negative = losing money

**But context matters:**
- Brand awareness campaigns may have lower immediate ROI
- Customer lifetime value extends the timeline
- Industry benchmarks vary"

---

**Slide 35-38: Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)**

"**CPA Formula:**
```
CPA = Total Campaign Cost / Number of Conversions
```

**Example:**
- Campaign cost: $3,000
- Conversions (purchases): 150

CPA = $3,000 / 150 = **$20 per customer**

**Is $20 good or bad?**
Depends on what you're selling!

- Selling $15 product: ❌ Bad (losing money)
- Selling $100 product: ✅ Good (80% margin)
- Selling $1,000 service: ✅ Excellent

**Compare to:**
- Cost of other channels (Google Ads, TV, etc.)
- Customer Lifetime Value
- Your profit margins"

---

**Slide 39-42: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)**

"**Why CLV Matters:**
That $20 customer might:
- Buy again next month
- Refer 3 friends
- Stay a customer for 5 years

**Simple CLV Calculation:**
```
CLV = Average Purchase Value × Purchase Frequency × Customer Lifespan
```

**Example: Subscription Box**
- Average order: $40
- Orders per year: 6
- Average customer stays: 2 years

CLV = $40 × 6 × 2 = **$480**

**Now Re-evaluate CPA:**
- If CPA is $20 to acquire
- And CLV is $480
- ROI over lifetime = (480 - 20) / 20 × 100 = **2,300%**

Suddenly that $20 CPA looks amazing!

**The Point:** Short-term ROI tells one story. Lifetime value tells another."

**1:35 - 1:55 PM: Lecture (20 min)**

**Slide 43-44: Why Reporting Matters**

"You can do the best analysis in the world, but if you can't communicate it effectively, it doesn't matter.

**Two Scenarios:**

**Scenario A: Bad Report**
'Here are 47 metrics from last month. Engagement was 5,234. Reach was 45,678. Impressions were 123,456...'
**Result:** Eyes glaze over. No action taken.

**Scenario B: Good Report**
'Our engagement rate increased 35% this month, driven primarily by video content. Recommendation: Shift 30% more budget to video production.'
**Result:** Clear action, easy decision.

**The difference?** Story, focus, and recommendations."

---

**Slide 45-48: Report Structure**

"Every good report has these elements:

**1. Executive Summary (One Paragraph)**
- What happened? (high level)
- Why does it matter?
- What should we do?

**Example:**
'Instagram engagement increased 35% in February, outperforming our target by 12%. This growth was driven by user-generated content campaigns and Reels. We recommend allocating an additional $2,000 to scale UGC incentives next month.'

**2. Key Metrics (3-5 Maximum)**
Not 20 metrics. Not 10. Maximum 5.
Focus on what matters to THIS audience.

**3. Insights (So What?)**
Don't just show numbers. Explain what they mean.
- ❌ 'Engagement increased 35%'
- ✅ 'Engagement increased 35% because we posted more Reels'

**4. Visualizations (Show, Don't Tell)**
- One clear chart worth 1,000 words
- Use principles from Day 2

**5. Recommendations (Now What?)**
Every report should end with: 'Based on this data, we should...'
Make it specific and actionable.

**6. Appendix (Details)**
Put the other 42 metrics here for people who want to dig in."

---

**Slide 49-52: Audience-Specific Reporting**

"Who you're reporting to changes EVERYTHING.

**CEO/Executive:**
- Time: 2 minutes max
- Focus: Business outcomes (revenue, ROI, growth)
- Details: Minimal
- Format: One page, executive summary
- Question they ask: 'Is this making money?'

**Marketing Director:**
- Time: 15-20 minutes
- Focus: Campaign performance, channel comparison
- Details: Moderate
- Format: 5-10 slides or pages
- Question: 'What's working and what needs to change?'

**Social Media Team:**
- Time: 30-60 minutes
- Focus: Tactical details, post performance, optimizations
- Details: High
- Format: Full dashboard, detailed breakdown
- Question: 'What should I post tomorrow?'

**Board/Investors:**
- Time: 5 minutes
- Focus: Strategic metrics, competitive position, ROI
- Details: Summary only
- Format: One slide in larger presentation
- Question: 'How does social support business strategy?'

**Key Principle:** More senior = less detail, more 'so what?'"

---

**Slide 53-55: Data Storytelling**

"Turn data into a story with three acts:

**Act 1: Setup (Context)**
'Last month, we set a goal to increase website traffic by 20%...'

**Act 2: Conflict/Change (The Data)**
'We achieved 35% growth, exceeding our target. However, Instagram drove 80% of this growth, while Facebook declined 10%...'

**Act 3: Resolution (Action)**
'We should reallocate $3,000 from Facebook to Instagram and test more Reels content.'

**Storytelling Elements:**
- Use before/after comparisons
- Highlight surprises ('Unexpectedly...')
- Show cause and effect ('Because we X, Y happened')
- Create tension ('While metric A improved, metric B declined')
- Always resolve with action"

---

**Slide 56-58: Common Reporting Mistakes**

"**Top 10 Report Killers:**

1. **Too Many Metrics** - More is not better
2. **No Context** - '6.8% engagement rate' means nothing without 'vs. 5.0% last month'
3. **No Recommendations** - Data without action is useless
4. **Jargon Overload** - 'Our CTR increased SOV driving CPA down' ← Nobody understands this
5. **Wrong Audience Level** - Sending detailed dashboard to CEO
6. **Ugly Design** - If it's hard to read, they won't read it
7. **No Clear Narrative** - Random stats thrown together
8. **Burying the Lede** - Put most important info FIRST
9. **Defensive Tone** - Own the bad news, explain it, fix it
10. **No Follow-Up** - Report and disappear vs. discuss and iterate

**Remember:** Your report competes with 50 emails, 10 meetings, and their phone. Make it worth their time."

**2:15 - 2:30 PM: Lecture (15 min)**

**Slide 59-60: Presenting to Executives**

"Presenting data is different from analyzing it.

**Key Principles:**

**1. Start with the Punchline**
Don't build suspense. Lead with your main point.
- ❌ 'So we looked at Q1 data, and analyzed engagement, and then we...'
- ✅ 'Instagram ROI increased 40% in Q1. Here's why and what we should do next.'

**2. The 2-Minute Rule**
Assume you have 2 minutes. Maybe you get 10. Maybe you get 30 seconds.
Always be able to deliver your core message in 120 seconds.

**3. Three Key Points Maximum**
Humans remember ~3 things. Structure:
- Point 1: Main finding
- Point 2: Supporting insight
- Point 3: Recommendation

**4. Anticipate Questions**
Prepare for: 'Why?', 'How much?', 'Compared to what?', 'What should we do?'"

---

**Slide 61-63: Presentation Structure**

"**The Perfect 2-Minute Pitch:**

**0:00-0:20 (Hook)**
'Our social media ROI increased 40% in Q1, generating an additional $50K in revenue.'

**0:20-1:00 (Evidence)**
'This was driven by three changes:
1. Shifted 50% of content to Reels (2× engagement)
2. Launched UGC campaign (30% lower CAC)
3. Optimized posting times (18% more reach)'

**1:00-1:30 (Insight)**
'The data shows video content and user-generated content significantly outperform static posts. Meanwhile, our audience is most active 6-9 PM, not during business hours like we previously assumed.'

**1:30-2:00 (Action)**
'I recommend we:
1. Allocate additional $5K to video production
2. Scale UGC incentive program
3. Shift posting schedule to evening hours

Expected outcome: 20% additional revenue growth in Q2.'

**Done. 2 minutes.**"

---

**Slide 64-66: Handling Questions**

"You will get questions. Be ready.

**Common Questions & How to Handle:**

**Q: 'Why did this happen?'**
A: Have hypothesis ready based on data. 'Our analysis suggests...' Show evidence.

**Q: 'How does this compare to competitors?'**
A: Have competitive benchmarks ready. 'Industry average is 3.8%, we're at 6.8%'

**Q: 'What if we do nothing?'**
A: Show trajectory. 'Based on trends, we'd miss Q2 target by 15%'

**Q: 'How much will this cost?'**
A: Have numbers ready. Investment, expected return, timeline.

**Q: 'Are you sure about this?'**
A: Confidence backed by data. 'Based on 3 months of consistent data showing...'

**If You Don't Know:**
'Great question. I don't have that data here, but I can get it to you by tomorrow afternoon.' 
Never make up answers."

**Slide 67-68: Presentation Mistakes to Avoid**

"**Don't:**
- ❌ Read slides word-for-word
- ❌ Apologize ('Sorry this is rough but...')
- ❌ Use filler words excessively ('um', 'like', 'you know')
- ❌ Hide behind slides (turn to screen, not audience)
- ❌ Get defensive about bad news
- ❌ Use technical jargon
- ❌ Talk too fast (nerves)

**Do:**
- ✅ Make eye contact
- ✅ Use confident body language
- ✅ Pause for emphasis
- ✅ Own the recommendations
- ✅ Acknowledge bad news directly, then pivot to solution
- ✅ Speak in plain language
- ✅ Breathe (seriously!)

**Practice Tip:** Record yourself. It's painful but effective."

**Slide 70-71: CASE STUDY 1 - Dove Real Beauty Campaign**

**Strategy:**

**Why It Worked:**

**Slide 72-73: CASE STUDY 2 - DiGiorno Pizza Twitter Fail**

**Failure Story**

**Background:**
- #WhyIStayed was trending on Twitter
- DiGiorno's social media manager saw the hashtag

**What They Did:**
- Tweeted: '#WhyIStayed You had pizza'
- Intended as humor about their pizza

**The Problem:**
- The hashtag was about domestic violence
- Women were sharing serious, traumatic stories
- DiGiorno co-opted a sensitive conversation for marketing

**Fallout:**
- Immediate backlash (thousands of angry responses)
- National news coverage (negative)
- Brand reputation damage
- Had to issue multiple apologies

**Why It Failed:**
- ❌ Jumped on trending hashtag without research
- ❌ Automation/quick posting without verification
- ❌ Tone-deaf to serious social issue
- ❌ Made brand look opportunistic and insensitive

**Lessons:**
- ALWAYS research a hashtag before using it
- Have approval processes for trending topics
- When in doubt, stay out
- Apologize quickly and sincerely when you mess up
- Not every trending topic is a marketing opportunity"

**Slide 74-75: CASE STUDY 3 - Glossier Community-Driven Growth**

**Success Story**

**Background:**
- Beauty brand launched in 2014

**Strategy:**

- Founder Emily Weiss blogged (Into the Gloss) for years
- Asked community what products they wanted
- User photos over professional models
- Every customer interaction = content opportunity
- Referral program (give $10, get $10)

**Results:**
- $0 spent on traditional advertising in first 3 years
- 70% of sales from peer referrals
- 2.5 million Instagram followers (highly engaged)
- Valued at $1.2 billion by 2019
- Engagement rate 5-10× higher than competitors

**Why It Worked:**
- ✅ Community first, product second
- ✅ Customers as co-creators (product development input)
- ✅ UGC as primary content (authentic)
- ✅ Incentivized referrals (word-of-mouth on steroids)
- ✅ Responsive to feedback (built trust)

**Lessons:**
- Community is more valuable than followers
- Co-creation builds investment and loyalty
- UGC + incentives = growth engine
- Patience—community building takes time
- Engagement rate > reach for sustainable growth"
